The day the job offer arrived, the house was unusually quiet.
The envelope was thin.
Plain paper.
No texture that stood out.
Still, the moment I opened it, the sound in the room felt like it stepped back.

“Railway Company. E
Those words stayed with me less for their meaning than for their shape on the page.

The next day, messages started coming in.

“You did it. E
“That’s a stable job. E
“A major company? E
Light messages. Quick approval.
On my phone screen, my name looked slightly more official, as if it had been edited by someone else.

The uniform felt heavier than it looked.
In the mirror, the lines across my shoulders appeared straighter than before.
When I adjusted the sleeves, the movement felt delayed, like the fabric resisted slightly.

I followed my senior into the station.
Inside, the air changed.
Cooler. Controlled.
Ticket gates clicked at steady intervals.
Announcements repeated without variation.
Footsteps crossed the floor in patterns that did not change.
The departure chime sounded again and again.

“From today, you’re on-site, Emy senior said.

We stepped onto the platform.
A train arrived.
People boarded.
People got off.
The same motion, repeating without visible difference.
Through the glass, I felt my posture tighten, as if I was being observed even when no one was looking directly at me.
Nothing unusual happened that day.
Only time passed.

For the first few weeks, the job was simple in definition.
Do not interrupt anything.
Do not delay anything.
Do not break the flow.
On the morning platform, people stood in the same positions every day.
Doors closed with the same sound.
Trains left after the same pause.
Staff stood between those moments, careful not to interfere with them.

Three months later, voices rose near the ticket gates.
At first, it sounded like laughter.
Then one voice pushed through the rest.
A staff member stepped in.

“Sir, over here please. E
The man smiled and walked closer, as if nothing was wrong.
Alcohol was in his breath. It spread slightly in the air near the gates.
People around stepped back a little—but not enough to leave.
Another staff member arrived and began speaking to him.
I could no longer clearly catch the words.
The explanation became noise. Then even noise lost structure.
Eventually, only sound remained. Then it faded.
The gates returned to their normal rhythm.

Another day, a drunk man grabbed my arm.
His fingers pressed into the fabric of the uniform, leaving a brief mark.
He let go immediately, without apology.
The train arrived on schedule.
The station clock did not change its pace.

“This happens sometimes, Esomeone said.

“We are on duty. E

No explanation. No escalation.

They did not move closer.
They did not leave either.
They simply stayed where they were, waiting for it to end.
Who was moving forward.
Who had stopped.
Only that sequence mattered.
The radio cracked with short messages.
Then even meaning began to break apart.
Only signal remained.
Eventually, the situation ended.
Someone left.
Someone else started walking again.
Nothing was left behind.
Only the shape of what had just ended.

After work, the break room was quiet.
The air conditioner ran without interruption.
I opened a can of coffee.
Before drinking, I checked the time.
Fourteen minutes left.

Same intervals. Same sound.
I held the cold can and watched.
Five minutes remained in the break.
The clock felt slightly out of sync, as if it was running behind the system it measured.
