## Chapter 1 — Off We Go

“Come on, everyone. We'll be leaving soon.”

Santa spread a large sack on the floor.

The shelves along one wall reached the ceiling. They were packed with stuffed animals, dolls, toy cars, and an old toy train.

Santa stood in front of them.

“Now then, what shall we do this year?”

He spoke to the train on the top shelf.

“Will you stay here again?”

“Yes. I want to stay and talk with everyone a little longer.”

“All right. Take care of things while I'm gone.”

A stuffed cat sat beside the train. Santa picked him up. The cat's fur was worn, and one ear had been sewn with blue thread.

“How about you?”

“I want to go.”

“Would you like to play with children again?”

“Yes. I'd like someone to hold me for a while.”

“Of course,” Santa said.

He held the cat in both hands and placed him gently in the sack.

Santa asked the other toys what they wanted. A small car chose to go, but a doll in a red hat decided to stay.

“Well, that's everyone, I think.”

Santa tied the sack and carried it over his shoulder. Outside, his reindeer were waiting.

“Sorry to keep you waiting. Let's go.”

Santa climbed into the sleigh. The reindeer ran across the snow, and soon the sleigh rose into the air, higher than the roof of his house.

Jingle, jingle.

The sound of the bells grew faint as the sleigh disappeared beyond the clouds.

* * *

Several months later, on a hot summer evening, Santa came out of a toy store with the toys he'd bought.

He wasn't wearing his red suit. In a light shirt and pants, with his long white beard, he looked like an ordinary old man.

He put his purchases into a bag, got into his sleigh, and flew away.

After a while, he saw a large building with a wide yard. Children's voices came from one of its windows.

Santa landed near the front door and took the stuffed cat out of the sack.

“Here we are.”

“Here?”

“Yes. There are lots of children here.”

The cat turned toward the door.

Santa placed him in a box with a few other toys and closed the lid.

“I hope you get to play a lot.”

“I hope so, too.”

Santa left the box beside the entrance and went back to his sleigh. The reindeer began to run.

Jingle, jingle.

* * *

Inside the children's home, the children were eating dinner.

“Hey, did you hear that?”

A little girl stopped with her spoon in the air.

“Hear what?” asked the boy next to her.

“Something outside. It sounded nice.”

The boy listened.

“Bells, maybe?”

“I think so.”

They looked out the window. A tree in the yard moved in the wind.

“I can't hear it anymore,” the boy said.

The girl nodded and went back to her dinner.

Later, one of the staff found a box by the front door.

“Someone must have left this here.”

After dinner, the children gathered around the box. There were several toys inside.

“Oh, a cat!”

The girl who had heard the bells picked up the stuffed cat.

“Look, he has blue stitches on his ear,” the boy said.

“Oh, yeah.”

She turned the cat over and looked at his back and tail.

“He's kind of old,” the boy said.

“Yeah.”

She held the cat against her chest.

“But he's cute.”

“There's more in here!”

The boy found a small toy car. He put it on the floor and sent it rolling across the room.

“Look how fast it goes!”

The girl bent down to watch, still holding the cat under one arm.

## Chapter 2 — Across the Sea

A cold wind blew through a town across the sea.

Santa put a large green cloak over his red suit. He looked at his reflection in a store window and smoothed his long white beard.

When he walked into a toy store, a clerk called from the back.

“Welcome!”

“Hi there. Can I look around?”

Santa walked along the shelves. He found a small robot, picked it up, and pressed the button on its back.

The robot took two steps, turned around, and fell over.

“Well, well.”

Santa stood it up. The robot took two more steps and fell again.

“That one falls over a lot,” the clerk said, laughing.

“It does, but it’s fun to watch.”

Santa tried the button again. This time the robot took three steps.

“Good job! Three steps this time!”

Santa bought it.

After he left the store, Santa heard a little voice from a box beside some trash in a side street.

Inside was a yellow toy car. One of its front wheels was missing.

“Oh, you've lost a wheel.”

Santa picked it up. Its roof was scratched, and there was a small sticker on one window.

“Would you like to drive again?”

“Yes!”

“Then come with me.”

He put the car in an old bag, away from the new toys he had bought.

* * *

The sleigh flew over the sea.

A large ship moved through the water far below. Nobody on the ship noticed the sleigh above them.

Jingle, jingle.

Santa watched the reindeer ahead of him as they flew to the next town.

It was so warm there that he began to sweat in his cloak. He took off the cloak and his red suit and changed into a light shirt.

“Whew! It's hot here.”

Before visiting the toy store, Santa bought some cold water and sat on a bench.

A boy was kicking a ball across the street. It rolled toward Santa, and he stopped it with his foot.

“Here you go.”

“Thanks!”

The boy picked up his ball and ran off.

Santa finished his water, then went to the toy store and bought a few soft balls.

* * *

That evening, Santa was going back to his sleigh when he saw an old wooden box beside the road.

A small toy piano was inside. Two of its keys were missing.

“Oh, a piano.”

Santa pressed one of the keys that was still there.

Plink.

He pressed another. It made a lower sound.

“That sounds nice. I could make you two new keys when we get home.”

He carried the piano to the sleigh. The yellow car was in the old bag.

“I've brought you a new friend,” Santa said.

He put the piano beside the car.

“Hello,” said the piano.

“Hello! I'm missing a wheel,” said the car.

“I'm missing two keys. Someone used to play lots of songs on me.”

“My kid took me everywhere.”

The two toys began talking in the bag.

Santa put the new toys into a different bag and climbed into the sleigh.

“Let's go to the next town.”

Jingle, jingle.

The reindeer set off across the sea.

## Chapter 3 — A Day by the Sea

Santa arrived in a town by the sea. He got down from his sleigh and stretched.

“What a beautiful day!”

Colorful umbrellas stood all along the beach, and many people were swimming. Santa took off his light shirt and put on his swim trunks. He tied his long white beard back before walking into the water.

“Ooh! It's cold!”

The waves moved him back and forth.

After swimming for a while, Santa came back to the beach and bought some ice cream.

“Hey, mister! There's ice cream in your beard,” said a boy nearby.

“Really?”

Santa brushed his beard, and the boy laughed.

“Thanks for telling me.”

When he had finished eating, Santa went back to the sleigh and got dressed.

“Let’s see what else is around here.”

He walked along the road by the sea until he came to a little square with several market stalls. Under colorful awnings, people were selling decorations made from shells, wooden dolls, and old toys.

“Oh, this looks fun.”

Santa bent down in front of one stall. A small tin crab stood on a wooden box.

“Does it move?”

“Yes,” said the man at the stall. “Turn the little key on its back.”

Santa turned the key and put the crab on the ground. It made a rattling sound as it walked sideways.

Santa laughed. “It moves like a real crab!”

Next to it was a little wooden boat with a white sail.

“Can this one float?”

“Of course. You can try it in that tub.”

Santa set the boat on the water. The wind caught its sail, and it slowly moved to the other side of the tub.

“What a nice little boat.”

He picked up the boat.

“I'll take the crab and the boat, please.”

Santa visited some other stalls and bought more toys. Soon he had a shopping bag in each hand.

“Oh dear. I've bought too much again!”

He laughed as he walked back to the sleigh.

“Come on, everyone. Let's go to the next town.”

The reindeer began to run, and the sleigh flew over the sea.

* * *

In the next town, Santa went looking for a toy store. As he turned at a busy intersection, he heard people cheering. A roller coaster raced past above the buildings.

“An amusement park!”

Santa stopped. The roller coaster rushed down a steep hill, and the people on it screamed.

“That looks like fun.”

He went into the park.

Santa rode the merry-go-round first, then the Ferris wheel. From the top he could see the sea beyond the town.

“Oh, that’s where I was earlier.”

After that, Santa joined the line for the roller coaster. He sat in the front seat, and an attendant lowered the safety bar.

“Are you sure you'll be okay, sir?”

“Of course. I've wanted to try this.”

The roller coaster began to move slowly.

Clickety-clack, clickety-clack.

As it climbed, Santa could see the town far below.

“What a view!”

Then the roller coaster went straight down.

“Waaaaah!”

Santa's long white beard flew up in the wind.

When the ride ended, he got out, feeling a little unsteady.

“Wow, that was fast!”

At the exit, Santa saw the ride photos. One showed him in the front seat with his hands in the air and his mouth wide open.

Santa laughed. “I want that picture!”

He bought a copy and put it in his bag.

After all the rides, Santa was hungry. He ate lunch at a restaurant in the park. By the time he came outside, the sun was getting low.

“Oh! I still need to visit the toy store.”

He left the park, found a toy store, and went inside. There were toys on every shelf. Santa picked them up and tried them out.

In one corner he found a small black stuffed cat. A ribbon was tied to one of its ears.

“What a cute cat.”

He decided to buy it too.

Santa chose several more toys and left the store carrying bags in both hands.

* * *

On his way back to the sleigh, Santa passed a small park. Some trash bags and old cardboard boxes were piled up nearby.

“Mister?”

Santa stopped.

“Hm?”

“Over here.”

The voice came from one of the boxes.

When Santa looked inside, he found a doll lying on her side. She was missing a leg.

He carefully picked her up.

“Oh, wait. I know you…”

Santa looked at her face.

“I brought you to a little girl three years ago, didn’t I?”

“Yes.”

“I didn’t expect to see you here.”

Santa smoothed the doll’s clothes. Stitches ran along the sleeves and front, in different colors of thread.

“Someone’s fixed your clothes quite a few times.”

“Yes.”

Santa sat the doll on his knee.

“What happened to your leg?”

“It came off.”

“I see.”

He looked in the box for her missing leg, but it wasn't there. Santa stood up.

“Do you want to come with me?”

“Yes.”

He put her in the old sack. A small voice came from inside.

“Hello!”

“Hello.”

“I'm a yellow car.”

“I'm a doll.”

“Are you missing a leg?”

“Yes. Just one.”

“I'm missing a wheel.”

Santa closed the sack. A bird was singing in a tree nearby.

“Well, it's time to go.”

He got into the sleigh, and the reindeer began to run.

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh rose into the evening sky above the town.

## Chapter 4 — Who Is It For?

Santa's sleigh landed in a wide meadow.

“Come on, everyone. Let's rest here for a while.”

The reindeer sniffed the grass and started eating. Santa climbed off the sleigh and stretched his back.

“We've bought a lot of toys!”

Large sacks of toys filled the sleigh. Santa sat down in the grass and opened one.

First, he took out the little robot that fell over after two steps. He pressed the button on its back. The robot walked forward, turned around, and fell again.

Santa laughed. “You're still doing that!”

He stood the robot up.

“I know a boy who would love you.”

Next he took out the tin crab from the market by the sea. He turned the little key on its back and put it in the grass. The crab walked sideways, making a rattling sound.

“I know someone who'll like you, too.”

He put the crab back. Santa looked at one toy after another, thinking of the children who would receive them. He smiled as he made his choices.

Near the bottom of the sack was a small black stuffed cat. It had a ribbon on one ear.

“Oh, there you are.”

Santa put the cat on his knee.

“Where should you go?”

After thinking for a moment, he nodded.

“Of course. I know where you'll go.”

He gently put the cat back in the sack.

* * *

When he'd finished looking at the toys, Santa lay down on the grass.

“I think I'll rest for a while.”

The wind moved through the grass. Santa closed his eyes and fell asleep.

After some time, something licked his cheek.

“Oh?”

He opened his eyes. A reindeer stood over him, looking down at his face.

“It's you!”

Santa sat up. Evening had come, and the light was fading.

“Have I been sleeping that long?”

He looked around. The other reindeer were still eating grass.

“You've all eaten a lot!”

The reindeer beside him pushed its nose against the sack.

“You can't eat that.”

The reindeer sniffed the sack again.

Santa laughed. “Still hungry?”

It walked back to the others. Santa tied up the sack.

December was almost here.

“Time to go home.”

He called the reindeer. They walked slowly back to the sleigh, full from all the grass they'd eaten.

“Let's go.”

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh rose above the meadow. It was dark now, and the sky was full of stars.

Soon they reached the sea. The dark water was calm, and the stars shone in it, too.

“What a beautiful night,” Santa said. “Look down at the water.”

The reindeer at the front looked toward the sea.

“Stars above and stars below. It's like we're flying among them.”

Santa smiled.

“But don't forget to watch where you're going!”

* * *

At last Santa was home. He carried the new toys inside and took them out of their sacks. Soon they covered nearly the whole floor.

“Oh my. That's a lot of toys!”

Santa laughed. He carried one of the empty shopping sacks to the laundry room. It was very dirty from the long trip.

He filled a tub with water and began to wash it.

“There we go.”

He rinsed the sack several times and hung it up to dry. Then he washed his travel clothes.

There was still an ice-cream stain on the light shirt he'd worn at the beach.

“Oh, it's still there.”

He rubbed the spot again.

By the time he finished washing everything, it was late at night.

When he went back to the room, the new toys were still spread across the floor. The old sack from his travels was lying beyond them.

Santa picked up one of the new toys.

“I hope the children like their presents.”

Outside, the reindeer were resting.

Christmas was almost here.

## Chapter 5 — Are Those Santa's Bells?

It was Christmas Eve.

Santa put on his clean red suit. In front of the mirror, he put on his hat and straightened his white beard.

“There. That looks good.”

The room was full of new toys. Santa checked every box before putting it into his big sack.

“Oh, I almost forgot.”

He picked up the small black stuffed cat with a ribbon on one ear.

“Oh, this box is for you.”

He put the cat into a different box and placed it in the sack.

Outside, the reindeer were waiting.

“I'm counting on you again this year.”

Santa climbed into the sleigh. The reindeer started running, and soon the sleigh was flying through the night.

Jingle, jingle.

Thick clouds covered the sky, and snow was falling.

“No stars tonight,” Santa said.

He held his hat as they flew on through the snow.

* * *

The town was decorated for Christmas. Colored lights shone in store windows, and lights were on in the houses.

Santa flew slowly over the streets.

Jingle, jingle.

Two sisters came out of a store and started walking home. The younger girl stopped.

“Did you hear that?”

“What?”

“Bells!”

Her older sister looked around.

“Maybe it's coming from a store.”

“No. It came from up there!”

The younger girl looked at the sky.

“Do you think it's Santa?”

Her sister looked up, too. Snow drifted through the air above them.

“Come on. Let's go home. It's cold.”

The younger girl watched the sky a moment longer. Then she followed her sister.

Jingle, jingle.

The bells grew quieter as they turned the corner.

* * *

That night, Santa flew past the children's home he'd visited in the summer. Small Christmas decorations hung in the windows.

Inside, the children were getting ready for bed.

“Hey,” a girl said to the child in the next bed.

“What is it?”

“Did you hear bells?”

They listened.

Jingle, jingle.

“I heard them!”

“Do you think that's Santa?”

“Maybe.”

The girl pulled her blanket up to her shoulders.

“Will we get presents tomorrow?”

“I hope so.”

A staff member called from the hallway.

“Time to go to sleep.”

“Okay!”

The girls closed their eyes.

That night, the girl fell asleep holding her stuffed cat and dreamed about Santa. He wore his red suit and carried a big sack over his shoulder.

* * *

The next morning, someone called from the hallway.

“Wake up, everyone! There are presents!”

The children ran out of their rooms. Boxes were waiting under the Christmas tree in the dining room.

“Wow!”

“Santa came!”

They began opening their presents. One child played with a new robot. Another wound up a tin crab and watched it walk sideways across the floor.

The girl who'd received the stuffed cat in the summer got a box, too. She was holding her old cat under one arm.

She opened the package and found a small black stuffed cat.

“Oh!”

A ribbon was tied to one of its ears.

“So cute!”

She looked at her old cat and put the black cat next to him.

“Here's your new friend.”

One cat had blue stitches in his ear. The other had a ribbon.

The girl picked up both cats and hugged them.

“Now the three of us can snuggle up every night!”

Nearby, a boy laughed as he played with the robot.

“Look! It fell over again!”

The girl turned to watch. Both cats watched with her.

## Chapter 6 — Santa's Shelf

Christmas was over, and Santa's sleigh landed in front of his snow-covered house.

“Home again. Good work, everyone.”

Santa patted each reindeer on the head and went inside.

“Whew, I'm tired.”

He sat down in his big chair and took off his red hat. After resting with his eyes closed, he got up again.

“Well, time to get to work.”

He put away the empty sack he'd used for Christmas presents. Then he brought over an old, dirty sack from the corner of the room. He had carried it on his travels.

When he opened it, the little yellow car peeked out.

“Hello there! How have you been?”

“Good! I've been talking with everyone.”

Santa put the car on his workbench. He found a new wheel that was the right size and put it where the old one was missing.

“Let's see you drive.”

The car rolled across the workbench.

“I did it!”

“That's it. You're rolling along nicely.”

Next was the little piano. Santa made new keys to replace the two that were missing. He made sure they were the same height as the others.

Plink, plonk.

“How does that sound?”

The piano played a short song, and Santa hummed along.

For a month after Christmas, Santa repaired the toys he'd found during his travels. He started work in the morning, took breaks for tea, and put his tools away at night.

* * *

One day, Santa took out the little doll who was missing a leg.

“Sorry to keep you waiting.”

He sat her on his workbench and took off her little outfit. Her arms and belly had been mended many times.

“What was it like living with that little girl?” Santa asked.

“I was with her every day.”

“Oh?”

“She kept me near her at meals and took me along when she went out. She carried me with her at bedtime, too.”

“Every night?”

“Yes. She always held me as she fell asleep.”

Santa picked up the doll's clothes.

“Did the girl do all this sewing?”

“No. Her mom did. The girl asked her to fix me.”

“She took good care of you.”

“Yes.”

Santa put thread through a needle.

“Every night,” he said. “That must have been nice.”

The doll watched him work for a while.

“Santa?”

“Yes?”

“She moved around a lot in her sleep.”

“Oh?”

“In the morning, I'd be on the floor all by myself!”

Santa laughed.

“Ha! That must have been quite a fall.”

“But she always picked me up when she woke up.”

“I see.”

Santa measured the leg she still had and started making a new one.

* * *

By the end of January, Santa had finished repairing the last toy.

He dressed the doll and sat her on the workbench with both feet together.

“There. All done.”

She tried moving her legs.

“Thank you, Santa.”

“You're welcome.”

He carried the doll to the big shelf. An old toy train sat among the toys already there.

“Oh, someone new!” the train called.

“Hello,” the doll said.

“You can sit here.”

There was room beside the train. Santa placed the doll there.

“There you go.”

“Where are you from?” the train asked.

“A town far away.”

“Really? I used to travel a long way, too.”

Santa listened to them as he put his tools in their box.

The train went on talking, and the doll answered him.

Santa looked up at the shelf one more time. Then he closed the door and went to bed.

**The End**
