## Chapter 1 — Off We Go

“Come on, everyone. It's almost time to go.”

Santa opened a big bag on the floor.

There was a tall shelf against the wall. It went all the way up to the ceiling. The shelf was full of toys: stuffed animals, dolls, little cars, and an old toy train.

Santa stood in front of it.

“Who wants to go this year?”

He spoke to the train at the top.

“Do you want to stay here again?”

“Yes. I want to stay and talk with my friends some more.”

“All right. Take care of the other toys for me.”

A stuffed cat sat next to the train. Santa picked him up. The cat's fur was worn. One ear had been sewn with blue thread.

“And you?”

“I want to go.”

“Do you want to play with children again?”

“Yes. I want someone to hold me for a while.”

“Of course,” said Santa.

He held the cat in both hands and put him into the bag.

Santa asked the other toys, too. A little car wanted to go. A doll with a red hat wanted to stay.

“I think we're ready.”

Santa tied the bag and put it over his shoulder. The reindeer were waiting outside.

“Sorry to keep you waiting. Let's go!”

Santa got into his sleigh. The reindeer ran across the snow. Soon the sleigh went up into the air, above the roof of the house.

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh flew into the clouds, and the bells grew quiet.

* * *

A few months later, it was a hot summer evening.

Santa came out of a toy store. He had bought some toys.

He was not wearing his red suit. He wore a light shirt and pants. With his long white beard, he looked like an ordinary old man.

Santa put the new toys in a bag and got into his sleigh.

After a while, he saw a big building with a large yard. He could hear children talking inside.

He landed near the front door and took the stuffed cat out of the bag.

“Here we are.”

“Here?” asked the cat.

“Yes. There are lots of children here.”

The cat looked at the door.

Santa put him in a box with some other toys. Then he closed the box.

“I hope you get to play a lot.”

“I hope so, too.”

Santa left the box by the front door and went back to his sleigh. The reindeer ran off.

Jingle, jingle.

* * *

The children in the home were eating dinner.

“Hey, did you hear that?” asked a little girl.

She stopped with her spoon in her hand.

“Hear what?” asked a boy next to her.

“Something outside. It sounded nice.”

The boy listened.

“Bells, maybe?”

“I think so.”

They looked out the window. A tree moved in the wind.

“I can't hear it now,” the boy said.

The girl nodded and went back to her dinner.

Later, a woman who worked at the home found a box by the front door.

“Someone left this here,” she said.

After dinner, the children came to see what was inside. There were several toys.

“Oh, a cat!”

The girl who had heard the bells picked up the cat.

“Look, there's blue thread on his ear,” the boy said.

“Oh, yeah.”

She turned the cat over and looked at his back and tail.

“He's kind of old,” the boy said.

“Yeah.”

The girl hugged the cat.

“But he's cute.”

“There's more in the box!”

The boy found a little toy car. He put it on the floor and pushed it across the room.

“It's fast!”

The girl bent down to watch. She still had the cat under one arm.

## Chapter 2 — Across the Sea

The wind was cold in a town across the sea.

Santa put a big green cloak over his red suit. He saw himself in a store window and touched his long white beard.

He went into a toy store.

“Welcome!” a clerk called from the back.

“Hello. Can I look around?”

Santa looked at the toys. He found a little robot and pushed the button on its back.

The robot took two steps, turned around, and fell over.

“Oh!” said Santa.

He stood it up. It took two steps and fell again.

“That robot falls a lot,” the clerk said, laughing.

“Ha! I like watching it,” Santa said.

He pushed the button again. This time the robot took three steps.

“Good job! Three steps!”

Santa bought the robot.

After he left the store, Santa heard a little voice. It came from a box beside some trash.

Inside was a yellow toy car. One front wheel was missing.

“Oh, you lost a wheel.”

Santa picked it up. There were marks on its roof and a little sticker on one window.

“Do you want to drive again?”

“Yes!”

“Come with me, then.”

Santa put the car in an old bag, away from the new toys.

* * *

The sleigh flew over the sea.

A big ship was on the water far below. The people on the ship did not see Santa.

Jingle, jingle.

Santa watched the reindeer ahead of him as they flew to the next town.

It was hot there. Santa was too warm in his cloak. He took off his green cloak and red suit and put on a light shirt.

“Whew! It's hot here.”

He bought some cold water and sat on a bench to drink it.

Across the street, a boy was kicking a ball. The ball rolled to Santa, and he stopped it with his foot.

“Here you go.”

“Thanks!”

The boy took his ball and ran off.

Santa finished his water, then went to a toy store and bought a few soft balls.

* * *

That evening, Santa saw an old wooden box by the road as he walked back to his sleigh.

A little toy piano was inside. It was missing two keys.

“Oh, a piano!”

Santa pushed one of the keys.

Plink.

He pushed another key. It made a lower sound.

“That sounds nice. I can make you two new keys when we get home.”

Santa picked up the piano and took it to the sleigh. The yellow car was in the old bag.

“Here's a new friend for you,” Santa said.

He put the piano next to the car.

“Hello,” said the piano.

“Hello! I'm missing a wheel,” said the car.

“I’m missing two keys. Someone used to play songs on me.”

“My kid took me everywhere.”

The two toys began talking in the bag.

Santa put the new toys in another bag and got into the sleigh.

“Let's go to the next town.”

Jingle, jingle.

The reindeer flew toward the land across the sea.

## Chapter 3 — A Day by the Sea

Santa came to a town near the sea. He got off his sleigh and stretched his arms.

“What a nice day!”

There were colorful umbrellas on the beach. Many people were playing in the water. Santa took off his light shirt and changed into his swim shorts. He tied his long white beard back and walked into the sea.

“Ooh, it's cold!”

The waves moved him from side to side.

After a swim, Santa went back to the beach and bought some ice cream.

“Hey, mister! There's ice cream in your beard!” a boy said.

“Really?”

Santa touched his beard. The boy laughed.

“Thank you!”

When he finished his ice cream, Santa went back to his sleigh and got dressed.

“I think I'll look around.”

He walked along the road near the sea. Soon he found a little market. Under bright umbrellas, people were selling things made from shells, wooden dolls, and old toys.

“This looks fun!”

Santa stopped at a small table. A little tin crab stood on a wooden box.

“Does it move?”

“Yes. Turn the little key on its back,” said the man at the table.

Santa turned the key and put the crab on the ground. The crab made a funny noise and walked sideways.

Santa laughed. “It walks like a real crab!”

Next to the crab was a small wooden boat with a white sail.

“Can this boat float?”

“Yes. Try it in that big tub of water.”

Santa put the boat in the water. The wind moved the white sail, and the boat went slowly to the other side.

“Oh, that's nice!”

He took the boat out.

“I'll buy the crab and the boat, please.”

Santa looked at the other tables and bought more toys. Soon he had a bag in each hand.

“Oh no! I bought too much again.”

He laughed and walked back to his sleigh.

“Come on, everyone. Let's go to the next town.”

The reindeer ran, and the sleigh flew over the sea.

* * *

In the next town, Santa went to look for a toy store. Then he heard people shouting happily. He looked up and saw a roller coaster moving fast behind some buildings.

“Oh, an amusement park!”

The roller coaster went down a big hill, and the people on it screamed.

“That looks fun!”

Santa went into the park. First he rode the merry-go-round. Then he went on the big Ferris wheel. At the top, he could see the sea far away.

“Oh! I was there earlier.”

Next Santa stood in line for the roller coaster. He sat in the front seat. A worker pulled down the bar in front of him.

“Are you sure you're okay, sir?”

“Of course! I want to try it.”

The roller coaster started moving slowly.

Clickety-clack, clickety-clack.

It went higher and higher. Santa could see the whole town below.

“What a great view!”

Suddenly, the roller coaster went down very fast.

“Waaaaah!”

Santa's long white beard flew up in the wind.

When it stopped, Santa got out. He had to stand still for a moment.

“Wow! That was fast!”

Near the exit, he saw photos from the ride. In one photo, Santa had both hands up and his mouth wide open.

Santa laughed. “What a funny picture!”

He bought the photo and put it in his bag.

Santa was hungry after all that fun, so he ate lunch at a restaurant in the park. When he came outside, the sun was getting low.

“Oh! I need to go to the toy store!”

He left the park and found a toy store. Inside, he tried many different toys.

In one corner, he saw a little black toy cat. It was soft, and it had a ribbon on one ear.

“What a cute cat!”

He bought the cat too, along with some other toys. Then he left with shopping bags in both hands.

* * *

On the way back to his sleigh, Santa walked past a small park. Near the park were some trash bags and old boxes.

“Mister?”

Santa stopped.

“Hm?”

“Over here.”

The voice came from a box.

Santa looked inside. A doll was lying there. She had lost one leg.

He picked her up carefully.

“Oh! I know you…”

He looked at her face.

“I brought you to a little girl three years ago, didn’t I?”

“Yes.”

“I didn’t think I’d find you here.”

Santa looked closely at the doll’s clothes. There were stitches on the sleeves and front. The thread was different colors.

“Someone fixed your clothes many times.”

“Yes.”

Santa put her on his knee.

“What happened to your leg?”

“It came off.”

“I see.”

He looked for the leg in the box, but it wasn't there.

“Do you want to come with me?” he asked.

“Yes.”

Santa put the doll in his old bag. Someone inside spoke to her.

“Hello!”

“Hello.”

“I'm a yellow car.”

“I'm a doll.”

“You lost a leg?”

“Yes. One leg.”

“I lost a wheel.”

Santa closed the bag. A bird was singing in a tree near the park.

“Time to go,” he said.

He got into his sleigh. The reindeer started running.

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh flew up over the town as evening came.

## Chapter 4 — Who Is It For?

Santa's sleigh came down in a big field of grass.

“Come on, everyone. Let's rest here.”

The reindeer sniffed the grass and began to eat. Santa got off his sleigh and stretched.

“We bought a lot of toys!”

There were big bags of toys in the sleigh. Santa sat on the grass and opened one.

First, he took out the little robot. It always fell over after two steps. Santa pushed the button on its back. The robot walked, turned around, and fell again.

Santa laughed. “You're still falling over!”

He stood the robot up.

“I know a boy who will like you.”

Next, Santa took out the little tin crab. He turned the key on its back. The crab walked sideways through the grass. It made a funny noise.

“I know someone who will like you, too.”

Santa put the crab back. He took out more toys and looked at them. He thought about the children who would get them.

At the bottom of the bag, he found a small black toy cat. There was a ribbon on one ear.

“Oh, there you are!”

Santa put the cat on his knee.

“Where should you go?”

He thought for a moment.

“Oh, I know who you should go to.”

He put the cat back in the bag.

* * *

Santa finished looking at the toys. Then he lay down in the grass.

“I'll rest here for a while.”

The wind moved the grass. Santa closed his eyes and went to sleep.

After some time, something licked his face.

“Oh?”

Santa opened his eyes. A reindeer was looking at him.

“Oh, it's you!”

He sat up. The sun was going down.

“Did I sleep for a long time?”

He looked around. The other reindeer were still eating.

“You ate a lot of grass!”

The reindeer next to him pushed its nose against the bag.

“You can't eat this.”

It sniffed the bag again.

Santa laughed. “Are you still hungry?”

The reindeer went back to the others. Santa closed the bag.

It was almost December.

“We need to go home.”

He called the reindeer, and they walked slowly back to the sleigh. Their stomachs were full.

“Let's go!”

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh went up into the sky. It was dark now. There were many stars above them.

Soon they flew over the sea. The water was quiet. Santa could see the stars on the water, too.

“Look! Isn't it beautiful? Look down there!”

The reindeer in front looked down.

“There are stars above us and stars below us. It's like we're flying through the stars.”

Santa smiled.

“Keep looking ahead, too!”

* * *

Santa came home after his long trip. He brought the new toys into the house. He took them out of their bags and put them on the floor. Soon there were toys everywhere.

“Oh! Look at all these toys.”

Santa laughed. He carried one of the empty shopping bags to the laundry room. The bag was very dirty.

He filled a big tub with water and washed the bag.

“There we go.”

He rinsed it again and again. Then he hung it up to dry. He also washed his clothes from the trip.

His light shirt still had an ice-cream stain from the beach.

“Oh, it's still there!”

He washed that part again.

It was late at night when he finished.

Back in the room, the new toys were still on the floor. The old bag from his trip was behind them.

Santa picked up one of the new toys.

“I hope the children like their presents.”

Outside the window, the reindeer were resting.

Christmas was almost here.

## Chapter 5 — Are Those Santa's Bells?

It was Christmas Eve.

Santa put on his clean red suit. He stood in front of a mirror, put on his hat, and fixed his white beard.

“There. I'm ready.”

There were new toys all around the room. Santa checked the boxes and put them in his big bag.

“Oh, that's right.”

He picked up a small black stuffed cat. It had a ribbon on one ear.

“This box is for you.”

He put the cat in the box and put the box in his bag.

The reindeer were waiting outside.

“Ready for another Christmas, everyone?”

Santa got into his sleigh. The reindeer began to run, and the sleigh went up into the night sky.

Jingle, jingle.

Clouds covered the sky. Snow was falling.

“I can't see the stars tonight,” Santa said.

He held his hat and flew through the snow.

* * *

The town was ready for Christmas. Lights shone in the store windows and inside the houses.

Santa flew over the town.

Jingle, jingle.

Two sisters came out of a store. As they walked, the younger girl stopped.

“Did you hear that?”

“What?”

“Bells!”

Her older sister looked around.

“Maybe it's from a store.”

“No! It came from the sky.”

The younger girl looked up.

“Is that Santa?”

Her sister looked up, too. She saw snow falling.

“Come on. Let's go home. It's cold.”

The younger girl looked at the sky again. Then she ran after her sister.

Jingle, jingle.

Soon the bells were far away.

* * *

That night, Santa flew past the children's home. He had been there in the summer. Small Christmas decorations hung in the windows.

Inside, the children were getting ready for bed.

“Hey,” one girl said to the child in the next bed.

“What?”

“Did you hear bells?”

They listened.

Jingle, jingle.

“Yes! I heard them.”

“Is it Santa?”

“Maybe.”

The girl pulled her blanket up to her shoulders.

“Will we get presents tomorrow?”

“I hope so.”

Someone called from the hall.

“Time to sleep!”

“Okay!”

The girls closed their eyes.

One of the girls had a stuffed cat in her arms. That night, she dreamed of Santa in his red suit, carrying a big bag.

* * *

The next morning, someone called from the hall.

“Wake up, everyone! There are presents!”

The children came out of their rooms. Many boxes were under the Christmas tree in the dining room.

“Wow!”

“Santa came!”

The children opened their gifts. One child played with a new robot. Another turned the key on a tin crab. The crab walked sideways on the floor.

The girl who got a stuffed cat in the summer got a box, too. She held her old stuffed cat under one arm.

She opened the box. Inside was a small black stuffed cat.

“Oh!”

It had a ribbon on one ear.

“So cute!”

She looked at her old cat and put the black cat beside him.

“Look, you have a friend!”

The old cat had blue stitches on one ear. The black cat had a ribbon.

She picked up both cats and held them close.

“Now the three of us can snuggle up every night!”

A boy nearby laughed at his robot.

“Look! It fell over again!”

The girl looked at the robot. Both cats watched, too.

## Chapter 6 — Santa's Shelf

Christmas was over. Santa and his reindeer came home. The sleigh landed in front of his house. Snow covered the ground.

“We're home! Thank you, everyone.”

Santa patted each reindeer on the head and went inside.

“I'm so tired.”

He sat in his big chair and took off his red hat. He closed his eyes and rested. Then he stood up.

“Well, back to work.”

Santa put away his empty Christmas bag. He got another bag from the corner. This old bag was dirty from his trip.

When he opened it, a little yellow toy car looked out.

“Hello! How are you?”

“Good! I talked with the other toys.”

Santa put the car on his worktable. It had lost a wheel. He found a wheel of the right size and put it on the car.

“Try it now.”

The car drove across the table.

“I did it!”

“Good! You can roll again.”

Next was the little toy piano. Two keys were missing. Santa made two new keys and put them in place.

Plink, plonk.

“How is that?”

The piano played a short song, and Santa hummed along.

For the month after Christmas, Santa fixed the old toys from his trip. He worked each day, stopped for tea, and put away his tools at night.

* * *

One day, Santa took out the doll with one leg missing.

“Sorry to keep you waiting.”

He sat her on his worktable and took off her clothes. There were old stitches on her arms and stomach. Someone had fixed her many times.

“Tell me about the little girl,” Santa said.

“I was with her every day.”

“Oh?”

“I sat beside her when she ate. She took me with her when she went out. At bedtime, she carried me with her.”

“Every night?”

“Yes. She held me as she fell asleep.”

Santa picked up the doll's clothes.

“Did the girl fix you?”

“No. Her mother did. The girl asked her to fix me.”

“She took good care of you.”

“Yes.”

Santa put thread through a needle.

“Every night,” he said. “That must have been nice.”

The doll watched his hands.

“Santa?”

“Yes?”

“The girl moved a lot when she slept.”

“Oh?”

“In the morning, I was on the floor!”

Santa laughed.

“Ha! That must have been a big fall!”

“But she picked me up when she woke up.”

“I see.”

Santa looked at the leg she still had. He measured it and began to make a new leg.

* * *

At the end of January, Santa finished fixing the last toy.

He put the doll's clothes back on her. Then he sat her on his worktable with both feet together.

“All done!”

She moved her legs.

“Thank you, Santa.”

“You're welcome.”

Santa took her to the big shelf. It was full of toys. An old toy train sat there, too.

“Oh, someone new!” said the train.

“Hello,” said the doll.

“You can sit here.”

There was a place beside the train. Santa put the doll there.

“There you are.”

“Where did you come from?” the train asked.

“A town far away.”

“I used to go far away, too.”

Santa put his tools in a box and listened to them talk.

The train kept talking, and the doll answered.

Santa looked at the shelf again. Then he closed the door and went to bed.

**The End**
