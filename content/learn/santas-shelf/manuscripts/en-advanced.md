## Chapter 1 — Off We Go

“Come on, everyone. It's almost time to go.”

Santa spread a large sack on the floor.

The shelves along one wall reached the ceiling. Stuffed animals, dolls, toy cars, and an old toy train filled every space.

Santa stood in front of the shelves.

“Well now, what shall we do this year?”

He turned to the train on the top shelf.

“Will you be staying here again?”

“Yes. I'd like to stay and talk with everyone some more.”

“All right. Keep an eye on things for me.”

Next to the train sat a stuffed cat. Santa lifted him off the shelf. The cat's fur was worn, and one ear had been stitched with blue thread.

“And you?”

“I want to go.”

“Would you like to play with children again?”

“Yes. I’d like someone to hold me for a while.”

“Of course,” Santa said.

Santa held the cat in both hands and tucked him into the sack.

He asked the other toys what they wanted. A small car chose to go, while a doll wearing a red hat decided to stay.

“Well, I think that's everyone.”

Santa tied up the sack and slung it over his shoulder. Outside, the reindeer were waiting.

“Sorry to keep you waiting. Let's go.”

He climbed into the sleigh, and the reindeer set off across the snow. Soon the runners lifted from the ground, carrying the sleigh above the roof of the house.

Jingle, jingle.

The bells faded as the sleigh disappeared beyond the clouds.

* * *

Months later, on a hot summer evening, Santa came out of a toy store with his purchases.

He wasn't wearing his red suit. In a light shirt and pants, with his long white beard, he looked like an ordinary old man.

He packed away the toys, climbed into his sleigh, and flew on.

After a while, he spotted a large building with a wide yard. He could hear children talking through one of the windows.

Santa landed by the front door and took the stuffed cat out of the sack.

“Here we are.”

“Here?”

“Yes. There are lots of children here.”

The cat turned toward the door.

Santa placed him in a box with several other toys and closed the lid.

“I hope you get to play a lot.”

“Me too.”

He left the box beside the entrance and went back to his sleigh. The reindeer started running.

Jingle, jingle.

* * *

It was dinnertime at the children's home.

“Hey, did you hear that?”

A little girl stopped with her spoon halfway to her mouth.

“Hear what?” asked the boy beside her.

“Something outside. It sounded nice.”

He listened.

“Bells, maybe?”

“I think so.”

They glanced out the window. A tree in the yard swayed in the breeze.

“I can't hear it anymore,” the boy said.

The girl nodded and went back to her dinner.

Later, one of the staff found the box by the front door.

“Looks like somebody left this here.”

After dinner, the children gathered around it. Several toys lay inside.

“Oh, a cat!”

The girl who had heard the bells lifted him out.

“Look, he has blue stitches on his ear,” the boy said.

“Oh, yeah.”

She turned the cat over to see his back and tail.

“He's kind of old,” the boy said.

“Yeah.”

She hugged the cat to her chest.

“But he's cute.”

“There's more in here!”

The boy found a small toy car, set it on the floor, and sent it rolling across the room.

“Wow, it's fast!”

The girl crouched to watch, the cat still tucked under one arm.

## Chapter 2 — Across the Sea

A cold wind blew through a town across the sea.

Santa pulled a large green cloak over his red suit. He checked his reflection in a store window and smoothed his long white beard.

When he entered a toy store, a clerk called from the back.

“Welcome!”

“Hello there. Mind if I look around?”

Santa browsed the shelves until a small robot caught his eye. He picked it up and pressed the button on its back.

The robot took two steps, spun around, and fell over.

“Well, well.”

Santa set it upright. It took another two steps and toppled over again.

“That one falls over all the time,” the clerk said with a laugh.

“I can see that. But it's fun to watch.”

Santa pressed the button once more. This time the robot managed three steps.

“Well done! Three steps this time!”

He bought the robot.

As Santa passed a pile of trash in a side street, a small voice called from a nearby box.

Inside was a yellow toy car with one of its front wheels missing.

“Oh, you've lost a wheel.”

He lifted it out. There were scratches on its roof and a little sticker on one window.

“Would you like to drive again?”

“Yes!”

“Then come along with me.”

Santa put the car in an old sack, apart from the new toys he'd bought.

* * *

The sleigh flew over the sea.

Far below, a large ship made its way across the water. Nobody on deck noticed the sleigh overhead.

Jingle, jingle.

Santa watched the reindeer ahead of him as they flew toward the next town.

The air there was so warm that he began to sweat beneath his cloak. He took off both the cloak and his red suit and changed into a light shirt.

“Whew! It's hot here.”

Before going to the toy store, he bought some cold water and sat on a bench to drink it.

Across the street, a boy was kicking a ball. When it rolled toward Santa, he stopped it with his foot.

“Here you go.”

“Thanks!”

The boy picked up his ball and ran off.

Santa finished his water, then went to the toy store and bought a few soft balls.

* * *

That evening, on his way back to the sleigh, Santa spotted an old wooden crate beside the road.

Inside was a little toy piano with two keys missing.

“Oh, a piano.”

He pressed one of the remaining keys.

Plink.

He tried another. This one made a lower note.

“That sounds nice. I could make you two new keys when we get home.”

Santa picked up the piano and carried it to the sleigh. The yellow car was already in the old sack.

“I've brought you a new friend.”

He placed the piano beside the car.

“Hello,” said the piano.

“Hello! I'm missing a wheel,” said the car.

“I'm missing two keys. Someone used to play all kinds of songs on me.”

“My kid took me everywhere.”

The two toys began talking inside the sack.

Santa packed the new toys into a separate bag and climbed into the sleigh.

“Off to the next town, then.”

Jingle, jingle.

The reindeer set off across the sea.

## Chapter 3 — A Day by the Sea

When Santa reached the seaside town, he climbed down from his sleigh and stretched.

“Ah, what a beautiful day.”

Bright umbrellas dotted the beach, and people were out enjoying the water. Santa took off his light shirt and changed into his swim trunks. He tied his long white beard back and waded into the sea.

“Ooh! That's cold!”

Each wave rocked him on his feet.

After a swim, he returned to the beach and bought some ice cream.

“Hey, mister! You've got ice cream in your beard,” said a boy nearby.

“Do I?”

Santa brushed his beard, making the boy laugh.

“Thank you for telling me.”

Once he'd finished eating, Santa went back to the sleigh and changed into his clothes.

“Let’s see what else is around here.”

Along the waterfront, he came upon a little square filled with market stalls. Beneath colorful awnings, vendors had laid out seashell decorations, wooden dolls, and old toys.

“Oh, look at all this.”

Santa crouched beside one of the stalls. A small tin crab stood on a wooden crate.

“Does this one move?”

“It sure does,” said the man running the stall. “Give the little key on its back a turn.”

Santa wound the key and set the crab on the ground. Off it went, rattling sideways.

“Ha! It walks like a real crab.”

Beside it was a wooden boat with a white sail.

“Will this one float?”

“Try it in that tub, if you like.”

Santa placed the boat on the water. A breeze caught the sail, carrying it slowly toward the far side of the tub.

“Oh, that's nicely made.”

He lifted it out.

“I'll take the crab and the boat, please.”

He stopped at a few more stalls and bought several other toys. By the time he was through, he had a bag in each hand.

“Ha! I’ve bought too much again.”

Santa carried his bags back to the sleigh.

“Come on, everyone. Next town!”

The reindeer broke into a run, and the sleigh lifted over the sea.

* * *

In the next town, Santa set off in search of a toy store. As he rounded a busy intersection, he heard cheering in the distance. A roller coaster shot past above the rooftops.

“An amusement park!”

He stopped to watch. The coaster plunged down a steep track, and the riders screamed.

“That looks like fun.”

Santa went to the park.

He rode the merry-go-round first, then climbed aboard the Ferris wheel. At the top, he could see the sea beyond the town.

“Oh, I was over there earlier.”

After the Ferris wheel, he joined the line for the roller coaster. He chose the front seat, and an attendant lowered the safety bar.

“Are you sure you'll be all right, sir?”

“Of course. I've been looking forward to this.”

The car began its slow climb.

Clickety-clack, clickety-clack.

From high above the park, Santa could see the town stretching away beneath him.

“What a view!”

Then the track dropped.

“Waaaaah!”

His white beard flew up in the wind.

When the ride ended, Santa stepped out on unsteady legs.

“Goodness, that was fast!”

At the exit, he spotted the ride photos. There he was in the front seat, both hands in the air, his mouth open wide.

Santa laughed. “I have to keep that one.”

He bought a print and slipped it into his bag.

After all that excitement, he was hungry. He had lunch at the park restaurant, and by the time he came outside, the afternoon sun had begun to dip.

“Oh! The toy store.”

Santa left the park. He found a toy store farther down the street and went in.

Shelves were crowded with toys. He picked them up one by one, trying out anything that caught his interest.

In a corner sat a small black stuffed cat, with a ribbon tied around one ear.

“Well, aren’t you cute!”

He decided to buy it, too.

Santa picked out several more toys and left with shopping bags in both hands.

* * *

On his way back to the sleigh, Santa passed a small park. Nearby, bags of trash and old cardboard boxes had been set out for collection.

“Mister?”

He stopped.

“Hm?”

“Over here.”

The voice came from inside a cardboard box.

Santa peered in. A doll lay on her side, one leg missing.

He lifted her carefully.

“Wait a minute. I know you.”

He held her up for a closer look.

“I brought you to a little girl three years ago, didn't I?”

“Yes.”

“I didn’t expect to find you here.”

Santa smoothed the doll’s clothes. The sleeves and front had been mended again and again, with different colors of thread.

“Someone’s mended your clothes a few times, haven’t they?”

“Yes.”

He sat her on his knee.

“What happened to your leg?”

“It came off.”

“I see.”

Santa searched the box, but the missing leg wasn't there. He got to his feet.

“Would you like to come with me?”

“Yes.”

He placed the doll in the old sack. A little voice rose from inside.

“Hello!”

“Hello.”

“I'm a yellow car.”

“I'm a doll.”

“You're missing a leg?”

“Yes. Just one.”

“I'm missing a wheel.”

Santa closed the sack.

A bird sang in a nearby tree.

“Well, we'd better be on our way.”

He climbed into the sleigh, and the reindeer set off.

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh left the town behind and climbed into the evening sky.

## Chapter 4 — Who Is It For?

Santa's sleigh settled in a wide meadow.

“Come on, everyone. Let's rest here for a while.”

The reindeer sniffed the grass and began to graze. Santa climbed down and stretched his back.

“Well, I certainly bought a lot.”

The sleigh was piled with sacks full of toys. Santa sat in the grass and opened one.

Out came the little robot that fell over after two steps. Santa pressed the button on its back. It marched forward, turned in a circle, and toppled over again.

“Ha! Still at it, I see.”

He stood the robot up.

“I know a boy who'd love to watch you do that.”

Next came the tin crab from the seaside market. Santa wound the key on its back and set it in the grass. The crab clattered sideways through the grass.

“Oh, I know someone who'll like you, too.”

He put the crab back. He tried another toy, put it away, and reached into the sack again. He could almost see the children's faces as he decided who should get what.

Near the bottom, he found the small black stuffed cat. A ribbon was tied to one ear.

“Oh, there you are.”

He rested the cat on his knee.

“Where should I take you?”

Santa thought for a moment, then nodded.

“Ah. I know who you should go to.”

He tucked the cat back into the sack.

* * *

Once he'd finished looking through the toys, Santa stretched out on the grass.

“I think I'll take a little rest.”

The wind stirred the grass around him. He closed his eyes and soon fell asleep.

Some time later, something licked his cheek.

“Oh?”

A reindeer was standing over him, its nose inches from his face.

“Ah, it's you.”

Santa sat up. The meadow had turned gold in the evening light.

“Well, well. Have I slept that long?”

He looked across the grass. The other reindeer were still eating.

“You've certainly had a good meal.”

The one beside him sniffed the open sack and nudged it with its nose.

“That's not for eating.”

The reindeer took another sniff.

Santa laughed. “Still hungry?”

It wandered back to the others, and Santa tied up the sack.

December was almost here.

“We'd better get home.”

He called the reindeer over. They made their way to the sleigh at an unhurried pace, full from their afternoon in the meadow.

“Let's go, then.”

Jingle, jingle.

The sleigh rose above the meadow. By now the sky was dark, and stars shone overhead.

Before long, they reached the sea. Stars wavered on the dark water below.

“Well, isn't that beautiful?” Santa said. “Look down at the sea.”

The lead reindeer glanced toward the water.

“Stars overhead and stars on the water. It's like flying right through them.”

Santa smiled.

“Ha! But keep your eyes on the way ahead!”

* * *

After his long journey, Santa carried the new toys inside, unpacking them until they covered nearly the whole floor.

“Well, this will take some sorting out!”

He chuckled, picked up one of the empty shopping sacks, and carried it to the laundry room. The long trip had left it grimy.

Santa filled a tub and set to work.

“Here we go.”

He scrubbed the sack, rinsed it several times, and hung it up to dry. Then he washed the clothes he'd worn on his travels.

The light shirt he had worn at the beach still had an ice-cream stain.

“Oh, that stain's still there.”

He gave it another scrub.

By the time he finished, it was late at night. Back in the room, the new toys lay scattered across the floor. Beyond them sat the old sack he'd carried on his travels.

Santa picked up one of the new toys.

“I wonder if they'll like what I've found for them.”

Outside the window, the reindeer were resting.

Christmas was almost here.

## Chapter 5 — Are Those Santa's Bells?

On Christmas Eve, Santa pulled on his freshly washed red suit.

He stood before the mirror to put on his hat and straighten his white beard.

“There. That should do.”

New toys filled the room. He checked the boxes one by one and packed them into his big sack.

“Oh, I nearly forgot.”

He picked up a small black stuffed cat. A ribbon was tied to one ear.

“Ah, here’s your box.”

He placed the cat inside and tucked the box into the sack.

The reindeer were waiting outside.

“I'm counting on you again this year.”

Santa climbed into the sleigh. The reindeer broke into a run, and the sleigh lifted into the night.

Jingle, jingle.

Heavy clouds covered the sky. Snow drifted past as they climbed.

“Well, no stars tonight,” Santa said.

He held on to his hat as they flew through the snow.

* * *

Christmas decorations lined the streets of the town below. Colored lights glowed in shop windows, and light shone from the houses.

Santa flew slowly over the rooftops.

Jingle, jingle.

Two sisters had just come out of a store. They were walking along together when the younger one stopped.

“Did you hear that?”

“Hear what?”

“Bells!”

Her older sister glanced around.

“From one of the stores?”

“No, up there!”

The younger girl tipped her head back.

“Do you think it was Santa?”

Her sister looked up, too. Snowflakes fell through the light from the shop windows.

“Come on. Let's get home. It's freezing.”

The younger girl kept watching the sky until her sister began to walk away. Then she ran to catch up.

Jingle, jingle.

By the time they turned the corner, the sound was growing faint.

* * *

Later that night, Santa passed the children's home he'd visited in the summer. Small Christmas decorations hung in the windows.

Inside, the children were getting ready for bed.

“Hey,” said a girl to the child in the next bed.

“What?”

“Did you hear bells?”

They listened.

Jingle, jingle.

“I heard them!”

“Could that be Santa?”

“Maybe.”

The girl pulled her blanket up to her shoulders.

“Do you think we'll get presents tomorrow?”

“I hope so.”

Someone called from the hallway.

“Time for bed, everyone.”

“Okay!”

The girls closed their eyes.

That night, the girl fell asleep holding the stuffed cat and dreamed of Santa. He was wearing his red suit, with a big sack over one shoulder.

* * *

The next morning, someone called down the hallway.

“Everybody, wake up! There are presents!”

Children came hurrying out of their rooms. Boxes were waiting beneath the Christmas tree in the dining room.

“Wow!”

“Santa came!”

Soon everyone was opening packages. One child tried out a new robot. Another turned the key on a tin crab and watched it scuttle across the floor.

The girl who'd been given the stuffed cat in the summer received a box, too. She had her old cat tucked under one arm.

She tore away the wrapping paper and opened the box. Inside was a small black stuffed cat.

“Oh!”

There was a ribbon on one ear.

“It's so cute!”

She looked at the cat under her arm, then placed the black one beside him.

“Look, you've got a friend!”

One cat had blue stitches in his ear. The other wore a ribbon.

She gathered them into her arms.

“Now the three of us can snuggle up every night!”

Nearby, a boy was laughing at the robot.

“Look! It fell over again!”

The girl turned to watch. The old cat and the black cat watched, too.

## Chapter 6 — Santa's Shelf

Christmas was over. Santa's sleigh came down in front of his house, where snow covered the ground.

“Home again. Good work, everyone.”

He gave each reindeer a pat on the head before going inside.

“Whew. I'm worn out.”

Santa sank into his big chair and took off his red hat. He closed his eyes for a while. Then he stood up.

“Well, there's work to be done.”

He put away his empty Christmas sack and brought out the old one from the corner of the room. It was the dirty sack he'd carried on his travels.

When he opened it, the little yellow car peeked out.

“Well, hello! How have you been?”

“Good! I've been talking with everyone.”

Santa set the car on his workbench. He found a wheel the right size to replace the missing one and fitted it into place.

“Go on. Let's see you drive.”

The car drove across the workbench.

“I did it!”

“That's it. You're rolling along nicely.”

Next came the little piano. Santa made replacements for its missing keys, making sure they sat level with the others.

Plink, plonk.

“How's that?”

The piano played a short tune, and Santa hummed along.

Over the next month, he worked his way through the toys he'd found on his travels. He started in the morning, broke for tea now and then, and put away his tools each night.

* * *

One day, Santa took out the doll with the missing leg.

“Sorry to keep you waiting.”

He sat her on the workbench and took off her little outfit. Her arms and belly bore the marks of repairs, sewn up again and again.

“What was it like, living with that little girl?” he asked.

“I was with her every day.”

“Oh?”

“She kept me beside her at meals and took me along when she went out. When it was time for bed, she carried me with her.”

“Every night?”

“She always held me as she fell asleep.”

Santa picked up her clothes.

“Did she do all this sewing herself?”

“No, her mom did. She asked her to fix me.”

“She must have taken good care of you.”

“She did.”

Santa threaded a needle.

“Every night,” he said. “That must have been nice.”

The doll watched his hands for a while.

“Santa?”

“Yes?”

“She used to toss and turn in her sleep.”

“Oh?”

“By morning, I'd be on the floor. All by myself!”

Santa laughed.

“Ha! That must have been quite a fall.”

“But she'd pick me up as soon as she woke.”

“I see.”

Santa measured the leg she still had and began making her a new one.

* * *

By the end of January, he'd repaired the last of the toys.

He dressed the doll and sat her on the workbench, her two feet side by side.

“There. All done.”

She tried moving her legs.

“Thank you, Santa.”

“You're welcome.”

He carried her over to the big shelf. An old toy train sat among the other toys.

“Oh, someone new!” the train called.

“Hello,” said the doll.

“There's a spot here.”

There was room for her beside the train. Santa set her down.

“There we are.”

“Where did you come from?” the train asked.

“A town far away.”

“Really? I used to travel a long way myself.”

Santa listened as he put his tools back in their box.

Up on the shelf, the train went on talking and the doll replied.

Santa looked up at the shelf once more. Then he closed the door and went to bed.

**The End**
