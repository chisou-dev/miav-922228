# Jeff — Episode 1: Arrival

Jeff's phone buzzes again.

**I have my bags. Where are you?**

Jeff writes back: **Near the exit for arriving passengers.**

**There are about six doors.**

**Pick one. I'll find you.**

**Very funny.**

Jeff puts his phone in his pocket. Before he can close his jacket, the phone makes another sound.

**How do I know which door to use?**

Jeff starts to write an answer. Then his friend comes through the doors. He pulls a large suitcase with one hand and holds his phone in the other.

Jeff calls his name and waves. His friend sees him. They hug, but the suitcase is between them.

“Man, I thought I'd never get out of there.”

“You made it.”

“Only just.”

Jeff steps back. His friend has a backpack over one shoulder. He holds the suitcase handle close to his chest.

“Did you bring everything you own?”

“Almost. I'm going to live here, remember?”

“You're staying at my place for two nights first.”

“Yeah, yeah. Then I get my own apartment.”

They walk farther into the terminal. After a few steps, his friend stops and looks around.

“Does Japan smell different to you?”

“Different how?”

“I don't know. It's just different.”

Jeff doesn't smell anything different.

The suitcase makes a noise on the smooth floor as they walk past some shops.

“This airport is so clean.”

“Are you hungry?”

“Very. They gave us something small to eat on the plane. I don't even know what it was.”

“Come on. We can eat here.”

They find a restaurant in the airport and put the suitcase next to their table. His friend looks at a menu with many pictures.

“Okay,” he says. “I've been studying Japanese. Let me order.”

“Are you sure?”

“I can do it.”

He puts up his hand to call the server.

“Sumimasen!”

The server comes to the table. His friend opens his mouth.

“Uh...”

He looks at the menu again.

“Um...”

Jeff waits.

“Okay. You order.”

Jeff points to a picture.

「かつ丼、二つお願いします」

The server says the order again. Jeff nods.

After the server leaves, his friend moves closer across the table.

“What did you say?”

“Two katsudon.”

“That's all?”

“That's all.”

“I knew the first part.”

“You did very well with the first part.”

“Shut up.”

His friend looks at the menu again.

“So what food did you order for me?”

“Fried pork on rice. There's egg on top.”

“Sounds good.”

“I think you'll like it.”

The food comes in two bowls. His friend looks closely at his bowl. The pork is in small pieces under soft egg. Some of the sauce is in the rice.

He takes a bite and keeps eating. Jeff breaks his wooden chopsticks apart.

“Oh, that's good.”

“Yeah?”

“That's really good. What's the sweet stuff?”

“The sauce.”

“I can taste that. What is it made from?”

“I don't know exactly.”

“You've been in Japan for three months.”

“That doesn't mean I know every sauce.”

Jeff is only halfway through his food when his friend's bowl is already empty.

“You eat fast.”

“I told you I was very hungry.”

His friend looks at the food still in Jeff's bowl.

“Can we get another one?”

“Another what?”

“That.”

“You just ate one.”

“And it was good.”

When Jeff finishes his food, his friend is looking at the menu again.

“Come on. One more.”

Jeff has never eaten two bowls of katsudon before. He looks at his empty bowl and calls the server back.

「すみません。かつ丼、もう二つお願いします」

Two more bowls arrive. Jeff eats more slowly now. His friend starts eating at once, but stops halfway through and puts down his chopsticks.

“Are you okay?” Jeff asks.

“Yeah. I just need a break.”

“You wanted another one.”

“I know.”

A few minutes later, they finish. His friend sits back and doesn't move.

“I think I made a mistake.”

“Really?”

“How do you feel?”

“About the same.”

They pay and leave the restaurant. His friend goes straight to a bench in the airport. Jeff sits next to him.

They watch people walk past with suitcases. Both of them wait for their stomachs to feel better.

“Do you eat two bowls all the time?”

“No.”

“Really?”

“This is my first time.”

“Then why did you order two?”

“You wanted another one.”

“I meant another one for me.”

Jeff turns to his friend.

“You could have told me that.”

His friend laughs, but stops and holds his stomach.

“Don't make me laugh.”

“Okay. How far is your place?”

“We have to take a train.”

“Can we take a taxi?”

Jeff looks at the suitcase, then at his friend.

“From here? Are you paying?”

His friend sits back against the bench.

“Okay. The train.”

# Jeff — Episode 2: The Apartment

When they get on the train, neither of them wants to talk about food anymore. Jeff's friend stands near the doors with a hand on his suitcase.

They find seats, but the suitcase is hard to fit between them. Every time they turn it, a wheel gets stuck.

“Here, give me your backpack,” Jeff says.

“I've got it.”

“I can see that. Give it to me.”

Jeff puts the backpack on his lap. His friend holds the suitcase between his knees.

The train starts moving. Across from them, several people have their eyes closed. Jeff's friend leans closer.

“How long are we on this train?”

“A while.”

“That's not a time.”

“I'll tell you when we have to get off.”

They change trains. At the next station, his friend tries to lift the suitcase over the space between the train and the platform. He almost drops it.

“This is crazy. How far away do you live?”

A few people turn to look at them.

“Not so loud,” Jeff says.

“What?”

“People are sleeping.”

His friend looks down the train, then turns to the window.

They pass apartment buildings and streets with shops. His friend notices a map above the doors.

“Wait. We're in Kanagawa?”

“Yeah.”

“I thought you lived in Tokyo.”

“I do.”

“Then why are we in Kanagawa?”

“The train goes through Kanagawa. Then we go back into Tokyo.”

His friend looks at the map again.

“That's a lot of trouble just to get home.”

“It's not usually this hard.”

“You don't usually have me and a suitcase.”

“Exactly.”

At last, they get off at Aihara. The station is very small compared with the others. Jeff walks past some parked bicycles and onto a quiet street.

“Is this still Tokyo?”

“Yep.”

“Are you sure?”

“Pretty sure.”

The wheels of the suitcase make a noise on the road as his friend catches up.

A few minutes later, they reach an apartment building. His friend stops at the bottom of the stairs.

“This is your place?”

“This is it.”

“It's old.”

“I know.”

“How much do you pay to live here?”

“Not much. My uncle owns it.”

“Your uncle?”

“He lives in Sagamihara. He lets me rent it cheap.”

His friend holds the rail and starts pulling the suitcase up the stairs.

“Does your uncle have an elevator somewhere?”

“Nope.”

At the apartment door, Jeff takes off his shoes and steps up into the room. His friend rolls the suitcase through the door and starts to walk in.

“Shoes,” Jeff says.

“What about them?”

“Take them off.”

His friend looks at Jeff's socks.

“Right here?”

“Yeah.”

“But I'm not inside yet.”

“You're in enough.”

He takes a step back and pulls off one sneaker. He stands on his other foot, then puts his bare foot on the lower floor near the door. Jeff points farther inside.

“Up here.”

“I'm trying.”

He takes off the other shoe, steps up into the room, and pulls the suitcase after him.

“What about the suitcase wheels?” he asks.

Jeff looks at the suitcase.

“Leave it here for now.”

The room is so small that they have to move out of each other's way. There is a low table near the middle and a folded futon against the wall. The small kitchen area is only a few steps from the suitcase.

His friend slowly turns around.

“Where's the bedroom?”

Jeff points at the floor.

“You're standing in it.”

“And the living room?”

“Same place.”

“Dining room?”

“Now you understand.”

His friend looks at the ceiling.

“I'm only staying for two nights, right?”

“That's what you told me.”

“Just checking.”

They move the low table to make space for another futon. While Jeff moves it, his friend sees two folders beside a shelf.

“What's all this?”

“Nothing. Put the table over there.”

But his friend is already looking inside one folder. It holds the flat front parts of instant ramen packages, in clear plastic pockets.

“Man. Do you ever eat anything but ramen?”

“You just saw me eat two bowls of katsudon.”

“Oh, right.”

He turns a page. The packages have different colors and pictures of bowls, noodles, and cartoon characters.

“Why do you keep the packages?”

“Only the front parts. They're all different.”

“How many do you have?”

“About seventy.”

“Seventy? You've only been here three months.”

“There are lots of different kinds.”

His friend looks at a few more pages, then opens the second folder. It has clean paper covers from disposable chopsticks. Some are plain. Others have restaurant names on them.

“You collect these too?”

“Yeah.”

“They're only wrappers. You're supposed to throw them away.”

“Some have different designs.”

“So you came all the way to Japan to collect things Japanese people put in the trash.”

Jeff holds out his hand for the folder.

“Are you done?”

“I'm just saying.”

“I know what you're saying.”

His friend gives it back. Jeff puts both folders on the shelf, farther away from the suitcase this time.

They put the two futons on the floor. They can walk between the futons and the table, but only if they turn sideways.

His friend sits on the edge of a futon and tries to stretch his legs.

“What do you do in here all day?”

“I'm usually at school or work.”

“I still can't imagine you working in a restaurant.”

“Neither can I.”

“What about when you're home?”

“Sleep. Eat. Sometimes go out.”

His friend looks toward the shelf.

“Collect trash.”

Jeff throws a pillow at him.

“You can sleep on the floor without a pillow, you know.”

His friend catches it and puts it behind his back.

“Okay, okay.”

After a moment, he gets up.

“Where's the bathroom?”

Jeff points to the narrow door next to the small kitchen area.

“In there.”

The door closes. Jeff is putting a sheet on his futon when he hears his friend call from the bathroom.

“Jeff!”

“What?”

“What the heck? The toilet's in the floor!”

# Jeff — Episode 3: A Day at Takao

Jeff wakes up at six. His friend is still asleep on the other futon, with one foot outside his blanket.

Jeff gets dressed and checks the train times. At seven, he bends down and gently pushes his friend's shoulder.

“Hey. Get up.”

His friend pulls the blanket over his head.

“No.”

“It's seven.”

“I got here yesterday.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you waking me up?”

“We're going to Mount Takao.”

“What's that?”

“A mountain. There's a chairlift.”

His friend pulls the blanket down.

“You should have told me that first.”

He sits up and checks the clock, then lies down again.

“Five more minutes.”

Jeff goes to put the kettle on.

On the way to the bathroom, his friend stops.

“Hey. That toilet. Are all toilets like that?”

“No, mostly just older places.”

“How did you learn to use it?”

“I asked my uncle.”

“You asked your uncle how to use a toilet?”

“You saw it.”

His friend looks toward the bathroom.

“Yeah, okay.”

They leave at about seven thirty. They change trains twice and reach Takaosanguchi just before nine. People with walking sticks and backpacks are already going toward the mountain.

“Those people look serious.”

“We're taking the chairlift.”

“All the way up?”

“Not all the way.”

His friend stops walking.

“How much farther after that?”

“I don't know. We'll see when we get there.”

The chairlift opens at nine. A worker tells them to come forward. A seat for two comes up behind them, and they sit down while it is still moving.

A little way up, his friend puts his phone deep inside his pocket.

“I'm not dropping that.”

Jeff looks down between their shoes. The trees are getting farther below them.

“Good idea.”

His friend holds the side of the seat tightly.

“Wow, it's higher than I thought.”

“But the view is nice.”

“Yeah. Just don't make me look straight down.”

“I think they take a photo on the way down,” Jeff said.

“Of us?”

“Yeah.”

“Okay, we need one of those.”

At the top, they walk along a paved path under the trees. The shade is cool, but the sun makes the path warm.

They pass a sign with pictures of monkeys by an entrance.

“Wait. There's a monkey park up here?”

“Yeah.”

“On a mountain?”

“I guess so.”

“Have you been?”

“No. The man I came with last time didn't go inside.”

“Can we go?”

“On the way back. Let's go to the temple first.”

His friend looks at the sign once more before he follows Jeff.

Farther along the path, they see a huge cedar tree. Thick roots twist around its trunk and come up out of the ground.

Jeff stops beside it.

“I remember this one.”

“What is it?”

“Look at the roots.”

His friend steps closer.

“They look like an octopus's arms.”

“Right? It's called Tako-sugi. Octopus Cedar.”

“That's a pretty good name.”

His friend walks around the tree to see the roots from the other side. Jeff waits while he takes a photo. Then they go on.

Red lights shaped like lanterns stand beside the path, between the trees. His friend slows down to look at one.

“Think these come on at night?”

“Maybe.”

“I bet they look great at night.”

Soon they come to two paths. Stone steps go up on the left, and an easier path turns right.

“Which way?”

Jeff checks the steps.

“This way, I think.”

“You think?”

“I came up these steps before. I remember that.”

“That's reassuring.”

“I followed a man from work. He knew the way.”

His friend tips his head back to look up the long staircase.

“More stairs. Great.”

Jeff puts one foot on the first step.

“Let's race to the top.”

His friend starts running up the steps before Jeff even finishes speaking.

“Hey, wait!”

Jeff runs after him. His friend runs fast at first but slows down halfway up. Jeff catches up and passes him.

“Come on!” Jeff called.

“I'm coming!”

At the top, Jeff stops with his hands on his hips, trying to breathe normally again. His friend arrives a moment later.

“No more racing,” he said.

“You started early.”

“You challenged me.”

After they get their breath back, they start walking again.

Tall wooden boards stand along the next part of the path. There are long lines of black Japanese writing on them. The two friends slow down to look.

“Whoa. That's a lot of writing.”

“Yeah.”

“What does it all say? Are these mountain rules?”

Jeff studies one board. He knows a few of the characters, but he still cannot understand what it says.

“I don't know.”

There are more boards farther along the path.

“That's a lot of rules.”

“If they are rules.”

They keep walking. Soon they see a large gate, with wooden buildings behind it.

His friend stops to take a photo.

“There's a shrine all the way up here?”

“I think it's a temple. Yakuoin.”

“How do you know?”

“That's what the man from work called it.”

Inside the gate, his friend stops several times to photograph the buildings. Jeff waits near the place where people are praying. When his friend joins him, they watch the people in front of them, then step forward and show respect in the same way.

As they leave, Jeff looks at the path that goes farther up the mountain.

“The top is about twenty more minutes away. Do you want to go?”

“You're serious?”

“We're pretty close.”

“I came here on a plane yesterday. You made me run up those stairs. I'm done.”

“Okay. We can head down.”

“Now that sounds good.”

They walk slowly on the way back. At the monkey park, Jeff follows his friend straight to the entrance.

Inside, a young monkey keeps bothering a bigger one. The bigger monkey chases it away, but after a few seconds it comes back.

His friend watches the monkeys longer than Jeff expects.

“Look. He's doing it again.”

The bigger monkey pushes the little one away again.

“Doesn't take a hint,” Jeff said.

“Nope.”

Near the exit, his friend finds a machine that sells small toys in capsules.

“What's in this?”

“I think they're pins with pictures of monkeys.”

He puts some coins in the machine and turns the handle. A capsule falls into a small tray. Inside it are two little pins with pictures of monkeys from the park.

He turns one pin over in his hand.

“What do you do with them?”

“Put them on your bag.”

“Or yours?”

“You're the one who bought them.”

He puts the pins away, still smiling.

They have been walking for hours when they get back to the chairlift. His friend sits down as soon as the next seat comes around.

On the way down, Jeff sees a camera in front of them.

“Oh, the picture.”

“Where?”

“Up there.”

A photographer calls out in Japanese. Jeff's friend gives a thumbs-up to the camera, but Jeff turns his head a second too late.

At the bottom, they find their photograph on a screen.

His friend starts laughing.

“Man, look at your face.”

“I was turning around.”

“What are you looking at?”

Jeff looks at the photograph again. His friend has his thumb up, but his eyes are closed.

“Your eyes are shut.”

His friend moves closer to the screen.

“Oh, man.”

He looks at the photograph for another moment.

“I'm getting it.”

“Seriously?”

His friend takes out his wallet.

He pays for the photograph. When he gets the printed photo, he looks at it once more, then puts it carefully into his backpack without bending it.

# Jeff — Episode 4: Plum Blossoms

As they leave the chairlift, Jeff's friend opens his backpack to check the photo he bought. He doesn't want to bend it. Along the street, Jeff buys two manju at one shop, rice crackers at another, and two rice balls farther down the road.

“You’re still hungry?” his friend asks, moving his backpack higher on his shoulder. “We just came down a mountain.”

“It's lunch.”

“Where are we eating?”

“By the river. Someone at work said the plum trees are blooming.”

“The what trees?”

“Plum trees. We can eat near the flowers. Do a little hanami.”

“You ever done that?”

“Not really.”

His friend looks at the bags Jeff is carrying. “Did you buy something to sit on?”

“No.”

“Then how are we going to eat?”

“We can eat while we walk.”

“Is that still a picnic?”

“No idea.”

They turn off the main road and walk between houses. Clothes hang outside behind low walls. Small trees reach over the street from the gardens. His friend stops now and then to take photos.

“You can’t see streets like this from the train,” he says.

“What are you taking pictures of?”

“Houses.”

“They're just houses.”

“Yeah. I know.”

Near the river, plum trees grow along the path. Most have small white flowers, and others have dark pink ones. Jeff waits while his friend takes a close-up photo of a branch.

“They're different colors.”

“Looks like it.”

Jeff takes the paper off one of the manju and gives it to his friend.

“What's inside?”

“Something sweet. I think there are beans inside.”

His friend takes a bite and looks at the inside.

“Sweet beans?”

“I guess so.”

He takes another bite, then holds the rest in his hand.

“Too sweet for me.”

“I've got rice crackers.”

“What's in those?”

“Rice.”

“More rice.”

Jeff breaks a rice cracker in two and gives his friend half. His friend eats it before Jeff can put the bag away.

“Okay. These are good.”

His friend puts the unfinished manju back in its paper.

They walk beside the river and stop at a railing to eat the rice balls. His friend follows the numbers on the plastic package. He pulls a thin strip down the middle, but the seaweed sticks to one side. A loose piece of clear plastic hangs from his hand.

“What am I supposed to pull?”

“Here. Give me that.”

Jeff pulls the rest of the plastic out from under the seaweed and folds the seaweed around the rice.

“How'd you do that?”

“You take off the plastic pieces in the right order.”

“It's like a magic trick.”

His friend takes a bite with the seaweed on it. He chews for a moment.

“I don’t really like the black part.”

“The seaweed?”

“Yeah. Can I take it off?”

He tries to take it off. The seaweed tears, and little pieces of rice stick to his fingers. Jeff is already taking the seaweed off his own rice ball.

“I don't really like it either,” Jeff said.

“You could've told me that.”

“You didn't ask.”

There is salmon inside Jeff’s rice ball. His friend eats the rice on his fingers and puts the torn seaweed on the empty package.

They finish eating by the river. The trees are full of flowers, but there are almost no leaves. A light wind moves the paper in his friend’s hand. He walks away to take another photo, with the rest of his rice ball in one hand and his phone in the other.

“Can’t you finish eating first?” Jeff calls.

“Where am I supposed to put it?”

When his friend comes back, Jeff holds out the bag for the empty packages.

They follow the path and see a wooden house behind a garden wall. The sign at the gate says *Takao Komagino Garden*. There is a pond inside, and the house behind it is much bigger than they expect.

“Can we go in?”

“Let's see.”

Carp move slowly through the pond. His friend waits at the water’s edge for an orange-and-white one to come close enough for a photo. Across the pond, ropes run from the top of a pine tree down to its branches.

“What are all those ropes for? Are they holding the tree up?”

“I don't know.”

“You don't know anything.”

“Hang on.”

Jeff finds a worker in the garden.

「すみません。あの松の縄、何のためですか」

「雪の重みで枝が折れないようにするんですよ」

「ありがとうございます」

He goes back to his friend and explains that the ropes protect the branches from heavy snow.

“Does it snow that much here?”

“Maybe sometimes.”

“Some tour guide you are.”

“I've only been here three months.”

They take off their shoes before going into the house. One room is set up for tea, with seats facing the garden. His friend takes out his phone while they read the menu.

“I'm ordering this time.”

“Go ahead.”

He types into a translation app and quietly reads the Japanese words on his screen.

“Matcha futatsu, yoroshiku onegaishimasu.”

He says the words again, more slowly. Then he practices two more times and puts his phone face down.

“Okay.”

“Ready?”

He puts up his hand when a staff member walks past the room.

「すみません！」

「はい」

His friend points to the matcha on the menu.

「抹茶二つ、よろしくお願いします」

「かしこまりました。少々お待ちください」

After the server leaves, his friend turns to Jeff. Jeff gives him a thumbs-up.

“Perfect.”

“It worked?”

“Two are coming.”

His friend gives Jeff a thumbs-up too.

A server brings two bowls of matcha and a small sweet for each of them. His friend takes a sip, then moves the bowl away from his mouth.

“Whoa. That's bitter.”

Jeff tastes his tea. “Yeah, it is.”

His friend eats the sweet and then drinks some more tea.

“Okay. You go back and forth.”

“Maybe that's the idea.”

After tea, they go outside and sit on a bench near the pond. His friend stretches his legs out.

“Are we done walking?”

“For a bit.”

A carp swims out from behind a rock. It moves near the top of the water, then disappears into a dark part of the pond. Another carp comes out. His friend watches them without taking out his phone.

“Hey, remember that festival when we were about ten?”

“Which festival?”

“The one where we went back to your house afterward.”

Jeff keeps watching the pond. “A little.”

“Your uncle and his wife were there. She made some kind of meat dish.”

“What kind?”

“It was in a pot. Kind of sweet. That was really good.”

“Was that Japanese food?”

“I thought so. She made it.”

Jeff tries to remember the food on the table, but he can’t remember what it was called.

“I'll ask my uncle sometime.”

“He lives nearby, right?”

“Sagamihara. It’s the next town.”

“Tell him I want some more of that.”

“Do you think he’ll remember?”

“I remember it.”

His friend watches the fish again. Someone walks along the gravel path behind them. The sound of the steps gets quieter as the person goes toward the house.

“You know,” his friend says after a while, “back home you would never sit in a garden all afternoon.”

“It's quiet.”

“I know.”

“You're not getting up either.”

“My legs won't let me.”

Jeff stays on the bench. His friend leans back and says nothing for a while.

It is after three when they finally stand up. They walk a short way down the road before his friend asks where they are going next.

“Hot springs,” Jeff said.

“There's more?”

“We can sit down there.”

“I’m in.”

The sun is low when they get back near Takaosanguchi Station. At a bathhouse near the station, they leave their shoes at the door and go into the changing room. His friend stops in front of the lockers.

“Wait. Where are the swimsuits?”

“There aren't any.”

“We go in naked?”

“Yeah.”

“Everybody?”

“Everybody.”

He looks around the room. “You could’ve told me.”

“Would you have come?”

“...Probably.”

Inside, he follows Jeff to the washing area. He washes himself and slowly gets into the bath. The water is so hot that he pulls up his shoulders at first. Soon he has both arms resting on the edge.

“Okay,” he said. “This is nice.”

“Told you.”

“My legs feel a lot better.”

“Mine too.”

Later, they order beer at the bathhouse restaurant. His friend takes a drink and puts down his glass.

“That was a good day.”

“Yeah, not bad.”

“We should do this every weekend.”

“Who's paying?”

“Was it that expensive?”

“The chairlift, the monkeys, the baths. And now the beer.”

His friend looks at his glass.

“Okay, once a month.”

“Even once a month is a lot.”

“Man, you're cheap.”

“You can pay next time.”

It is dark when they leave. They take the train from Takaosanguchi and change trains at Takao for the Chuo Line. His friend closes his eyes as soon as he sits down.

Jeff touches his shoulder as the train gets near Hachioji.

“Hey. Wake up. We have to change.”

“Huh? Already?”

“Keep sleeping and you'll end up at Tokyo Station.”

His friend opens his eyes.

“Aren't we in Tokyo now?”

“Tokyo Station. Come on.”

The train stops at Hachioji. They get up and step onto the platform.

# Jeff — Episode 5: The Maze

“Push down here.”

Jeff pushes down on the suitcase while his friend tries to close it. The zipper stops halfway, and a shirt sleeve sticks out.

“It was closed when you got here,” Jeff says.

“Yeah. I don't know what's wrong with it.”

Jeff pushes the sleeve back inside. He keeps his weight on the lid until his friend can close the zipper. Then they carry the suitcase down the stairs of Jeff's building.

His friend is moving to Shinjuku today. Before he left the United States, he found an apartment with furniture through his Japanese language school. He has to be at the school at eleven. He can get his apartment keys this afternoon.

On their way to Aihara Station, his friend asks, “Hey, what do you say when you go to work?”

“At the restaurant? Usually *otsukaresama desu*. Everyone says it.”

“Ot-su-ka-re-sa-ma-desu.”

“Why?”

“I'm going to try it at school. I need something to say.”

They walk on.

They take the Yokohama Line to Hashimoto, then change to the Keio Line. His friend keeps the suitcase between his feet and reads an email from the school.

“It says the school is fifteen minutes from the west exit.”

“Then we're fine.”

“Do you know where that exit is?”

“We'll find it.”

They arrive in Shinjuku a little after nine.

People get off the train and hurry toward the stairs. Jeff follows them, but before he and his friend reach the ticket gates, they stop. Many signs hang above them. One points toward the west exit, so they follow it. Soon they reach another place where the paths go in different directions.

“I think it's this way.” Jeff walks ahead.

His friend pulls the suitcase toward another passage. “Isn't west over there?”

“The sign doesn't say that.”

“Okay. You lead.”

They follow the passage under the station, climb some stairs, and come out on a street. His friend looks at the map on his phone. The blue dot is on the other side of the station from the school.

“Wrong side.”

“Yeah.”

“What now?”

Jeff looks at the stairs they just used. “We go back down.”

There are more people under the station now. The suitcase wheels keep getting stuck between the floor tiles. His friend carries it for a few steps, puts it down, and pulls it again.

“Hey. Didn't we see that man before?” His friend grabs Jeff's sleeve.

“Who?”

“The man with the black bag.”

“Maybe he just looks like someone we saw.”

“No. I'm pretty sure it's him.”

“Maybe he thinks he's seen us before, too.”

His friend turns to look, but the man is already gone in the crowd.

“How do people get out of here? Seriously.”

The passage divides in two again. Jeff reads the signs above them.

“This place is like a dungeon in a video game.”

“Every time we turn, there's another hallway.”

“And when we finally get outside, we're in the wrong place.”

「はい」

“Thanks.”

# Jeff — Episode 8: The Book

On his day off, Jeff takes the wrinkled paper wrapper from his collection binder. It used to hold a pair of chopsticks.

Last night he tried again and again to fold it into a small rest for chopsticks. Now the paper is flat, but there are lines everywhere. He can't remember which fold came first. He tries again, and the paper opens before he can put the chopsticks on it.

On his phone, he finds a video that shows how to make one. He stops the video to see the next step, but the person's fingers are in the way. He goes back a few seconds and watches again. Now he can't remember how the paper needs to look before that step.

Jeff closes the video. Maybe a book with pictures of every step is easier to use.

He decides to look in a bookstore in Hashimoto.

Hashimoto is one train stop from Aihara. After he leaves the station, he goes into a large shopping center and looks at the list of stores to find the bookstore.

He's looking for books about folding paper when he sees a book cover he knows in the manga section.

It's a comic book series his friend used to read all the time back in America. The books here are in Japanese.

There's a copy of the first book that customers can read. Jeff picks it up. The words are in Japanese, but he can understand more than he expects. He doesn't know all the kanji, and the pictures help him follow the story.

He only wants to read a few pages. But he keeps reading, standing beside the shelf. Sometimes he goes back a page to check something he doesn't understand.

When he checks his phone, he sees that almost an hour has passed.

He can actually read a lot of the book.

He closes it. Books two through ten are next to the first one.

Maybe he can send them to his friend.

Jeff puts the copy he is reading back on the shelf and picks up all ten books. The pile is heavier than he thinks.

He's heading for the checkout when he remembers why he came here.

With the manga in his arms, he checks the store map and finds the section for arts and crafts. Some books have pictures of paper birds and flowers. Others show boxes and small things made from folded paper. He looks through a few of them.

One book shows how to make a rest for chopsticks.

It isn't exactly like the paper rests from the table for six at the restaurant, but it's made from the same kind of wrapper. There's a picture of the finished rest, with drawings beside it to show each step.

He follows the drawings with his eyes, one after another. They look easy enough.

On another page, there's a small container made from a paper napkin. Jeff stops for a moment, then adds the book to his stack.

At the front of the store, he asks the cashier to send the ten manga books to his friend's home in Shinjuku. The cashier gives him a form. Jeff writes the address, pays for the books and the delivery, and leaves with the folding book in a bag.

Back in his apartment, he puts the book and the old chopstick wrapper on his low table. He opens the book to the right page and puts the paper beside the first drawing.

There's a Japanese word next to it that he doesn't know: 谷折り.

He checks it on his phone. *Valley fold.* The same page has another word, 山折り. His phone says *mountain fold*.

He folds the paper in half, opens it, and turns it over to look at the line in the paper.

He follows the arrows in the book, but on the third picture he folds the wrong way. Then his finger slips as he tries to push one corner inside.

He opens the wrapper and begins again.

The old lines in the paper make it hard, but this time he can push the end into place. The little rest stays up on the table. He puts a pair of disposable chopsticks on it, and they stay there, too.

For a while, he looks at his work and the photo in the book. His rest is wider, with one side that isn't straight. But it's close to the picture.

Jeff opens his collection binder. He keeps many chopstick wrappers inside. Some have restaurant names on them, and others have designs.

He begins to take one out, but stops. It's a nice, clean wrapper, with no folds in it.

He puts it back in the binder and moves his old, wrinkled chopstick rest near the edge of the table. Then he turns to the next page of the book.

# Jeff — Episode 12: Tokyo at Night

# Jeff — Episode 13: The Mikoshi

「祭りだからな」

Jeff drinks some of the water. The back of his neck is already wet with sweat.

「は？」

Jeff thinks of someone standing on the head of a statue of Jesus in a church, without taking off their shoes. He doesn't like the idea.

Someone calls the man, and he walks around to the other side.

The cook laughs.

The cook points to Jeff as he speaks.

The cook is telling his family that Jeff has been working very hard lately. He says he can count on Jeff, too.

The cook turns back to Jeff when he hears that.

Then he leaves with his family to speak to someone else.

# Jeff — Episode 14: My Town

They reach the top a little after eight thirty. There is an empty bench, so they sit down.

“We made it.”

“That was a lot of climbing.”

His friend takes out his bread while Jeff takes the wrapper off a rice ball.

“Food tastes better up here.”

“You haven't even taken a bite.”

“I can tell.”

His friend takes a bite of his bread.

They look out from the top for a while. Then his friend takes out his phone and finds their chairlift photo from the first visit. In it, his thumb is up but his eyes are closed. Jeff is looking away from the camera.

“You still have that?” Jeff asks.

“Good picture, isn't it?”

“Look at us. We look ridiculous.”

“Let's take the lift down and try for a good one.”

“Thought we were walking the whole way.”

“We walked up. That's enough.”

Jeff finishes his rice ball.

They walk down past Yakuoin, taking the same way as on their first visit. His friend goes slowly where tree roots cross the path.

“Going down's harder on my legs than going up.”

“Not much farther to the lift.”

“Didn't you just say that?”

They get on the chairlift together. On the way down, his friend reaches for his phone, then leaves it in his pocket.

“Where was that camera?”

“Should be coming up.”

Jeff sees the camera ahead.

“Keep your eyes open this time.”

“And you face the camera.”

They both look at the camera as their chair passes it.

At the bottom, they find their picture on a screen. This time, both are smiling with their eyes open.

“I'm getting this one,” Jeff says.

His friend takes out his wallet.

“I want one too.”

“You bought one last time.”

“Yeah, but that was last time.”

“Okay, let's get two.”

They buy two copies of the same picture. His friend puts his in his backpack. Jeff puts his where it won't bend.

“I might put this up in my room when I get home.”

“What about the old one?”

“That one's going up too.”

“With us looking like that?”

“It's a good picture.”

Outside the chairlift station, Jeff checks the train times on his phone.

“Want to go to the same hot spring as last time?”

“Found another place. Let's try that one.”

“Is it far?”

“We take a train, then walk a bit.”

“No more hills, okay?”

“I think it's flat.”

They take a train and walk to the bathhouse. His friend looks around as he takes off his shoes.

“Think we'll have the place to ourselves at this hour?”

“Don't count on it.”

There are only a few people at the washing stations when they go in.

“See? Hardly anyone here.”

“Not exactly a private bath.”

After their baths, they eat a late lunch at the bathhouse restaurant. His friend finishes his cold green tea and looks at the clock.

“I might go back in.”

“We've got time.”

They rest for a while, then go in for another bath.

After their second bath, his friend points toward the restaurant.

“I could go for a beer.”

“We just ate there.”

“Just a drink this time.”

They go back to their table and order a beer each.

“That felt good,” he says, putting down his glass. “Man, I could live here. I'd come every day.”

“Live at the bathhouse?”

“Near it.”

“You'd come every day?”

“Even just on my days off would be nice.”

His friend takes another drink.

“Wouldn't mind more days like this.”

“You planning to climb a mountain every weekend?”

“The mountain part? Once in a while.”

They stay at the table after their beers are gone. When they leave, they've been at the bathhouse for about three hours. There are more people there now.

His friend falls asleep almost as soon as he sits down on the train.

Near the station where they need to change trains, Jeff taps his friend on the shoulder.

“Hey, wake up. We have to change here.”

His friend opens his eyes.

“Are we there?”

“We're changing trains.”

He picks up his backpack, stands, and follows Jeff out.

---

On Sunday, Jeff is standing in front of the fish tank when his friend wakes up.

“Looking at them again?”

“One of the eggs is gone.”

His friend bends down beside him and looks at the plants.

“Maybe it's stuck somewhere else.”

“Could be.”

They can't find the egg. Jeff stands up, and his friend gets up too.

“Come on, let's get lunch. I'm starving.”

His friend packs his things for the trip home. Then they leave and walk toward the station.

Just around the corner, a man with a shopping bag raises his hand and calls Jeff by name. Jeff sees this man most mornings on his way to the station.

「おう、ジェフ。この前はお疲れさん」

「お疲れさまです」

「また祭りも頼むよ」

The man laughs and walks on.

His friend watches him go.

“You know that guy?”

“We carried the mikoshi together at the festival.”

“You joined a festival out here, too?”

“Yeah. My shoulder was killing me afterward.”

His friend gives Jeff a quick pat on the shoulder.

“Worse than the mountain?”

“Way worse.”

Outside the supermarket, the woman who works at the register sees Jeff and calls to him from near the door.

「あ、ジェフ！　今日はお友達？」

「はい」

「いつものラーメン、新しいの入ったよ」

「ほんとですか。帰りに見ます」

「またね」

Jeff waves, and she goes back inside.

“They even know you at the grocery store?”

“I go there all the time.”

“She even knows what you buy.”

“I buy the same stuff every time.”

Before they reach the main road, somebody calls Jeff's name from the other side of the street.

「ジェフー！」

It's one of the regular customers at the izakaya. He raises a hand as he keeps walking, and Jeff waves back.

His friend stops.

“What are you, famous around here?”

“No. They're just people I see all the time.”

“I've been here two days, and nobody's called my name once.”

“You don't live here.”

“Fair point.”

His friend starts walking again, then turns to Jeff.

“So what's for lunch?”

“There's a katsudon place nearby.”

“Katsudon again?”

“We can go somewhere else.”

“No, katsudon's fine. Just one bowl this time.”

“One's enough for me too.”

“Wasn't that the plan last time, too?”

Jeff laughs and points farther down the street.

“This way. Just a little farther.”

“After all that walking yesterday?”

“It's close.”

His friend adjusts the strap on his backpack and follows Jeff.
