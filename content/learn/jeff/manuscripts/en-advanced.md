# Jeff — Episode 1: Arrival

Jeff's phone buzzed again.

**Got my bags. Where are you?**

He typed back: **By the arrivals exit.**

**There are like six doors.**

**Then pick one. I'll find you.**

**Very funny.**

Jeff put his phone away, but it buzzed before he'd finished zipping his jacket.

**How do I know which side to come out on?**

He was still typing when his friend came through the doors, dragging a suitcase with one hand and holding his phone in the other.

Jeff called out and waved. His friend spotted him, and they managed a hug around the suitcase.

"Man, I thought I'd never get out of there."

"You made it."

"Barely."

Jeff stepped back. His friend had a backpack slung over one shoulder and the suitcase handle pulled almost to his chest.

"You bring everything you own?"

"Pretty much. I have to live here, remember?"

"Two nights at my place first."

"Yeah, yeah. Then I get a real apartment."

They headed farther into the terminal, but after a few steps his friend slowed to take a look around.

"Is it just me, or does Japan smell different?"

"Different how?"

"I don't know. Just different."

Jeff couldn't smell anything unusual.

The suitcase rattled over the polished floor as they passed the shops.

"This airport is crazy clean."

"You hungry?"

"Starving. They gave us this little thing on the plane. I don't even know what it was."

"Come on. There's somewhere we can eat."

They found a restaurant in the terminal and left the suitcase beside the table. His friend flipped through a menu covered in photographs.

"All right," he said. "I've been practicing my Japanese. Let me handle this."

"You sure?"

"I've got it."

He lifted a hand toward the server.

"Sumimasen!"

The server came over. His friend opened his mouth.

"Uh..."

He glanced at the menu again.

"Um..."

Jeff waited.

"Okay, you order."

Jeff pointed to one of the pictures.

「かつ丼、二つお願いします」

The server repeated the order, and Jeff nodded.

After the server left, his friend leaned across the table.

"What'd you say?"

"Two katsudon."

"That's it?"

"That's it."

"I knew the first part."

"You did great with the first part."

"Shut up."

He went back to the menu.

"So what did you order me?"

"Fried pork cutlet on rice. There's egg on top."

"Sounds good."

"I think you'll like it."

When the bowls arrived, his friend leaned closer to the food. The pork had been cut into strips under a layer of soft egg, and the rice had soaked up some of the sauce.

He took a bite and kept chewing while Jeff pulled his chopsticks apart.

"Oh, that's good."

"Yeah?"

"That's really good. What's the sweet stuff?"

"The sauce."

"I can taste that. What is it?"

"I don't know exactly."

"You've been here three months."

"Doesn't mean I know what's in every sauce."

Jeff had barely made it halfway through his own bowl when the one across the table was empty.

"You eat fast."

"I told you I was starving."

He nodded toward the food Jeff had left.

"Can we get another?"

"Another what?"

"That."

"You just had one."

"And it was good."

By the time Jeff finished, his friend was checking the menu again.

"Come on. One more."

Jeff hadn't eaten two bowls of katsudon before. He looked at the empty dish in front of him, then called the server back.

「すみません。かつ丼、もう二つお願いします」

The second bowls arrived. Jeff was slower this time. Across the table, his friend started eating right away but put his chopsticks down halfway through.

"You okay?" Jeff asked.

"Yeah. Just taking a break."

"You wanted another one."

"I'm aware."

A few minutes later, they finished. His friend leaned back and stayed there.

"I might've made a mistake."

"You think?"

"How are you feeling?"

"About the same."

They paid and left the restaurant. His friend made straight for a bench along the concourse, and Jeff sat down beside him.

For a while they watched people pass with suitcases, each of them waiting for his stomach to settle.

"You eat two bowls all the time?"

"No."

"Seriously?"

"First time."

"Then why'd you order two?"

"You wanted another one."

"I meant for me."

Jeff turned toward him.

"You could've mentioned that."

His friend laughed, then stopped and held his stomach.

"Don't."

"Okay. How far is your place?"

"We've got a train ride."

"Can we take a cab?"

Jeff looked at the suitcase, then at him.

"From here? You paying?"

His friend sank back against the bench.

"Train it is."

# Jeff — Episode 2: The Apartment

By the time they reached the train, neither of them was talking about food anymore. His friend stood by the doors with one hand on the suitcase handle.

They found seats, but fitting the suitcase between them took some work. Every time they turned it, one of the wheels caught on something.

"Here, give me your backpack," Jeff said.

"I got it."

"I can see that. Give me the backpack."

Jeff took the backpack and held it on his lap while his friend wedged the suitcase between his knees.

The train pulled away. Across the aisle, several passengers had their eyes closed. His friend leaned toward him.

"How long are we on this thing?"

"A while."

"That's not a time."

"I'll tell you when we need to get off."

They changed trains. At the next station, the suitcase nearly slipped out of his grip as he tried to lift it over the gap.

"This is ridiculous. How far out do you live?"

A few passengers turned toward them.

"Keep it down," Jeff said.

"What?"

"People are sleeping."

He glanced down the car, then turned toward the window.

They rode on past apartment buildings and shopping streets. The route map above the doors caught his friend's attention.

"Wait. We're in Kanagawa?"

"Yeah."

"I thought you lived in Tokyo."

"I do."

"Then why are we in Kanagawa?"

"The train goes through Kanagawa. We go back into Tokyo."

He looked back at the map.

"That seems like a lot of work just to get home."

"It isn't usually this much work."

"You don't usually have me and a suitcase."

"Exactly."

They finally got off at Aihara. Compared with the stations they had come through, this one was tiny. Jeff led the way past a row of parked bicycles and onto a quiet street.

"This is still Tokyo?"

"Yep."

"You sure?"

"Pretty sure."

The suitcase wheels rattled as he caught up with Jeff.

It took them several more minutes to reach the apartment building. His friend stopped at the bottom of the stairs.

"This is it?"

"This is it."

"It's old."

"I know."

"How much do you pay for a place like this?"

"Not much. My uncle owns it."

"Your uncle?"

"He lives over in Sagamihara. Lets me stay here cheap."

His friend grabbed the stair rail and started hauling the suitcase up.

"Does your uncle have an elevator somewhere?"

"Nope."

At the apartment door, Jeff took off his shoes and stepped up onto the floor inside. His friend rolled the suitcase through the doorway, then started to follow it.

"Shoes," Jeff said.

"What about them?"

"Take them off."

He glanced at Jeff's socks.

"Right here?"

"Yeah."

"But I'm not even in yet."

"You're in enough."

He backed up and pulled off one sneaker while balancing on the other foot. When he put his bare foot down on the lower floor by the door, Jeff pointed past him.

"Up here."

"I'm trying."

The second shoe came off. He stepped up into the room and dragged the suitcase after him.

"What about the wheels?" he asked.

Jeff looked at the suitcase.

"Just leave it here for now."

The room was so small that they had to get out of each other's way just to move around. A low table stood near the middle, with a folded futon pushed against the wall. The kitchenette was only a few steps from where they'd left the suitcase.

His friend turned slowly in place.

"Where's the bedroom?"

Jeff pointed to the floor.

"You're standing in it."

"And the living room?"

"Same place."

"Dining room?"

"You're getting it."

He looked up at the ceiling.

"I'm only here two nights, right?"

"That's what you told me."

"Just checking."

They had to shift the low table to make room for the second futon. As Jeff moved it, his friend spotted two folders tucked beside a shelf.

"What's all this?"

"Nothing. Put the table over there."

But his friend had already opened one. Inside were flattened fronts from instant ramen packages, arranged in clear sleeves.

"Dude. Have you eaten anything besides ramen?"

"You just watched me eat two bowls of katsudon."

"Oh, right."

He turned another page. The packages were different colors, with drawings of bowls, noodles, and cartoon characters.

"Why are you keeping the bags?"

"Just the fronts. They're all different."

"How many do you have?"

"About seventy."

"Seventy? You've only been here three months."

"There are a lot of kinds."

His friend went through a few more pages, then opened the second folder. This one held clean paper sleeves from disposable chopsticks, some plain and others printed with restaurant names.

"You collect these too?"

"Yeah."

"They're just wrappers. You're supposed to throw them away."

"Some of them have different designs."

"So you came all the way to Japan to collect stuff Japanese people throw in the trash."

Jeff held out his hand for the folder.

"You done?"

"I'm just saying."

"I know what you're saying."

He handed it back. Jeff returned both folders to the shelf, farther from the suitcase this time.

They laid out the two futons. There was just enough room to walk between them and the table if they turned sideways.

His friend sat on the edge of one and tried stretching his legs.

"What do you do in here all day?"

"I'm usually at school or work."

"I still can't picture you working at a restaurant."

"Neither can I."

"What about when you're home?"

"Sleep. Eat. Sometimes go out."

His friend glanced toward the shelf.

"Collect trash."

Jeff tossed him a pillow.

"You can sleep on the floor without one, you know."

He caught the pillow and tucked it behind him.

"Okay, okay."

After a moment he stood up.

"Where's the bathroom?"

Jeff pointed toward the narrow door beside the kitchenette.

"In there."

The door closed. Jeff was pulling a sheet over his futon when a voice came from the bathroom.

"Jeff!"

"What?"

"What the hell? The toilet's in the floor!"

# Jeff — Episode 3: A Day at Takao

Jeff woke up at six. His friend was still asleep on the other futon, one foot sticking out from under the blanket.

He got dressed and checked the train times. At seven, he leaned over and nudged his friend's shoulder.

"Hey. Get up."

His friend pulled the blanket over his head.

"No."

"It's seven."

"I got here yesterday."

"I know."

"Then why are you waking me up?"

"We're going to Mount Takao."

"What's that?"

"A mountain. There's a chairlift."

He lowered the blanket.

"You could've led with that."

He sat up, looked at the clock, and lay back down.

"Five more minutes."

Jeff went to put the kettle on.

On his way to the bathroom, his friend stopped.

"Hey. That toilet. Are they all like that?"

"No, mostly just older places."

"How'd you figure out how to use it?"

"I asked my uncle."

"You asked your uncle how to use a toilet?"

"You saw it."

His friend glanced toward the bathroom.

"Yeah, okay."

They left around seven thirty. After a couple of train changes, they arrived at Takaosanguchi just before nine. People with hiking poles and backpacks were already walking toward the mountain.

"Those people look serious."

"We're taking the lift."

"All the way up?"

"Not all the way."

His friend stopped walking.

"How much farther after that?"

"I don't know. We can see when we get there."

The lift started running at nine. An attendant waved them forward, and a two-person chair came around behind them. They sat down as it kept moving.

A little way up, his friend tucked his phone deep into his pocket.

"I'm not dropping that."

Jeff looked down through the gap between their shoes. The trees were getting farther away.

"Good idea."

His friend gripped the side of the seat.

"Man, it's higher than I thought."

"Pretty nice view, though."

"Yeah. Just don't make me look straight down."

"I think they take a photo on the way down," Jeff said.

"Of us?"

"Yeah."

"Okay, we need one of those."

At the top, they followed a paved path under the trees. It was cool in the shade, but warm whenever the sun reached the path.

They passed an entrance with pictures of monkeys on a sign.

"Hold on. There's a monkey park up here?"

"Yeah."

"On a mountain?"

"Apparently."

"Have you been?"

"No. The guy I came with last time didn't go in."

"Can we go?"

"On the way back. Let's get to the temple first."

He took one more look at the sign before following Jeff.

Farther along stood a huge cedar. Its roots twisted out around the trunk, thick enough to look like they belonged above the ground.

Jeff stopped beside it.

"I remember this one."

"What is it?"

"Look at the roots."

His friend stepped closer.

"Those look like tentacles."

"Right? They call it Tako-sugi. Octopus Cedar."

"That's a pretty good name."

He went around to see the roots from the other side. Jeff waited while he took a picture, then they moved on.

Red lantern-shaped lights stood along the path between the trees. His friend slowed beside one.

"Think these come on at night?"

"Maybe."

"Bet that looks cool."

Soon the path split. Stone steps went up on the left; a gentler path curved away to the right.

"Which way?"

Jeff looked at the steps.

"This way, I think."

"You think?"

"I came up these stairs before. I remember that much."

"That's reassuring."

"I was following a guy from work. He knew where he was going."

His friend tipped his head back to look up the staircase.

"More stairs. Great."

Jeff put his foot on the first one.

"Race you to the top."

Before Jeff had finished speaking, his friend was already running up the steps.

"Hey, wait!"

Jeff ran after him. His friend was fast for the first stretch but slowed halfway up. Jeff caught him and went past.

"Come on!" Jeff called.

"I'm coming!"

At the top, Jeff stopped with his hands on his hips, trying to get his breath back. His friend arrived a moment later.

"No more racing," he said.

"You started early."

"You challenged me."

They set off again once they'd caught their breath.

A row of tall wooden plaques lined the next stretch of path. Black characters ran down them in long columns. They slowed to look.

"Whoa. That's a lot of writing."

"Yeah."

"What's it all say? Rules for the mountain?"

Jeff studied one of the boards. He recognized a few characters, but they didn't help much.

"Couldn't tell you."

More of the boards stretched along the path.

"That's a lot of rules."

"If they're rules."

They kept walking. A large gate came into view, with wooden buildings beyond it.

He stopped to take a picture.

"There's a shrine all the way up here?"

"A temple, I think. Yakuoin."

"How do you know?"

"That's what the guy from work called it."

Inside the gate, his friend kept stopping to photograph the buildings. Jeff waited by the area where people were praying. When his friend caught up, they watched the visitors ahead of them, then stepped forward and paid their respects.

On the way out, Jeff glanced at the path continuing uphill.

"The top of the mountain's still another twenty minutes or so. Want to go?"

"You're serious?"

"We're pretty close."

"I flew here yesterday. You made me run up those stairs. I'm good."

"Okay. We can head down."

"Now you're talking."

They took their time on the way back. At the monkey park, Jeff followed him straight to the entrance.

Inside, one young monkey kept pestering a larger one. It would get chased away, wait a few seconds, then come right back.

His friend stayed in front of the enclosure longer than Jeff expected.

"Look. He's doing it again."

The larger monkey gave the little one another shove.

"Doesn't take a hint," Jeff said.

"Nope."

Near the exit, his friend found a capsule machine.

"What's in this?"

"Looks like pins. With the monkeys on them."

He put in some coins and turned the handle. A capsule dropped into the tray. Inside were two small pins with pictures of monkeys from the park.

He turned one over in his hand.

"What do you do with these?"

"Put them on your bag."

"Or yours?"

"You bought them."

He put the pins away, still grinning.

By the time they reached the chairlift, they'd been walking for hours. His friend sat down as soon as the next chair came around.

On the way down, Jeff spotted a camera ahead of them.

"Oh, the picture."

"Where?"

"Up there."

A photographer called out in Japanese. Jeff's friend gave the camera a thumbs-up; Jeff turned a second too late.

At the bottom, they found their photograph on the display.

His friend started laughing.

"Dude. Look at your face."

"I was turning around."

"What are you even looking at?"

Jeff took another look. His friend had his thumb up, but his eyes were closed.

"Your eyes are shut."

His friend leaned closer.

"Oh, man."

He looked at the photograph for another moment.

"I'm getting it."

"Seriously?"

His friend took out his wallet.

He paid for the photograph. When the print was handed over, he studied it once more, then slipped it into his backpack, careful not to bend it.

# Jeff — Episode 4: Plum Blossoms

As they left the chairlift, his friend opened his backpack to make sure the photo he'd bought wasn't getting bent. Jeff stopped at a shop along the street for two manju, picked up some rice crackers next door, and bought a couple of rice balls a little farther along.

“You're still hungry?” his friend asked, shifting his backpack on his shoulder. “We just came down a mountain.”

“It's lunch.”

“Where are we eating?”

“Down by the river. A guy at work said the plum trees are blooming.”

“The what trees?”

“Plum trees. Figured we could eat by the blossoms. Do a little hanami.”

“You ever done that?”

“Not really.”

His friend checked the bags in Jeff's hands. “Did you get something to sit on?”

“No.”

“Then how's this gonna work?”

“We can eat while we walk.”

“Is that still a picnic?”

“No idea.”

They left the main road and followed a street between houses. Laundry hung behind low walls, and branches from the small gardens reached over the pavement. Every so often, his friend stopped for a picture.

“You don't see streets like this from the train,” he said.

“What are you taking pictures of?”

“Houses.”

“They're just houses.”

“Yeah. I know.”

Near the river, plum trees lined the path. Most had small white blossoms, but some were a darker pink. Jeff stopped to wait while his friend took another picture, this time up close to one of the branches.

“They're different colors.”

“Looks like it.”

Jeff opened the paper around one of the manju and passed it over.

“What's inside?”

“Something sweet. I think it's beans.”

His friend bit into it and examined the filling.

“Sweet beans?”

“Apparently.”

He tried another bite, then held the rest in his hand.

“Too sweet for me.”

“I've got rice crackers.”

“What's in those?”

“Rice.”

“More rice.”

Jeff broke one and handed him half. His friend finished his half before Jeff had put the bag away.

“Okay. These are good.”

His friend wrapped up the unfinished manju.

A little farther on, the path ran beside the river. They stopped by the railing to eat the rice balls. His friend followed the numbers printed on the plastic wrapper and pulled a narrow strip down the middle. The seaweed stuck to one side, leaving him with a loose sheet of clear plastic.

“What am I supposed to pull?”

“Here. Give me that.”

Jeff slid the remaining plastic out from under the seaweed and folded it around the rice.

“How'd you do that?”

“You pull the pieces off in order.”

“It's like a magic trick.”

His friend took a bite, seaweed and all. He chewed for a moment.

“I'm not crazy about the black part.”

“The seaweed?”

“Yeah. Can I take it off?”

He tried peeling it back. It tore, bringing bits of rice with it, which stuck to his fingers. Jeff was already pulling the seaweed off his own rice ball.

“I don't really like it either,” Jeff said.

“You could've told me that.”

“You didn't ask.”

There was salmon inside Jeff's. His friend ate the rice stuck to his fingers and collected the torn pieces of seaweed on the wrapper.

They finished eating beside the river. The branches were full of flowers, with hardly any leaves yet, and the breeze kept rattling the paper in his friend's hand. He wandered off to photograph another tree, holding what was left of his rice ball in one hand and his phone in the other.

“Couldn't you finish eating first?” Jeff called.

“Where am I supposed to put it?”

When he came back, Jeff held out the bag for the empty wrappers.

They followed the path until a wooden house appeared beyond a garden wall. A sign at the gate read *Takao Komagino Garden*. Through the entrance they could see a pond, and the house behind it was much larger than either of them had expected.

“Can we go in?”

“Let's see.”

Carp moved slowly through the pond. His friend went down to the edge and waited for a big orange-and-white one to come close enough for a picture. On the far side, ropes stretched from high above a pine tree down to its branches.

“What's all that string for? Are they tying the tree up?”

“I don't know.”

“You don't know anything.”

“Hang on.”

Jeff found a member of the garden staff nearby.

「すみません。あの松の縄、何のためですか」

「雪の重みで枝が折れないようにするんですよ」

「ありがとうございます」

He came back and explained about the snow weighing on the branches.

“Does it snow that much here?”

“I guess sometimes.”

“Some tour guide you are.”

“I've only been here three months.”

They took off their shoes to enter the house. One of the rooms had been set up for tea, with seats overlooking the garden. While they were reading the menu, his friend took out his phone.

“I'm ordering this time.”

“Go ahead.”

He typed into a translation app and read the Japanese on the screen under his breath.

“Matcha futatsu, yoroshiku onegaishimasu.”

He tried it again, slower. A third time, then once more, before turning the phone facedown.

“Okay.”

“Ready?”

He raised his hand to catch the attention of a staff member passing the room.

「すみません！」

「はい」

His friend pointed to the matcha on the menu.

「抹茶二つ、よろしくお願いします」

「かしこまりました。少々お待ちください」

Once the server had gone, he turned to Jeff. Jeff gave him a thumbs-up.

“Perfect.”

“It worked?”

“Two are coming.”

He returned the thumbs-up.

A server brought them two bowls of matcha and a small treat each. His friend took a sip and pulled the bowl away from his mouth.

“Whoa. That's bitter.”

Jeff tried his. “Yeah, it is.”

His friend ate the sweet, then took another sip of tea.

“Okay. You need to go back and forth.”

“Maybe that's the idea.”

After tea they went back outside and sat on a bench by the pond. His friend stretched his legs out in front of him.

“Are we done walking?”

“For a bit.”

A carp came out from behind a rock, swam just under the surface, and disappeared into the darker water on the other side. Another took its place. His friend watched them without taking out his phone.

“Hey, remember that festival we went to when we were, like, ten?”

“Which festival?”

“The one where we went back to your house afterward.”

Jeff kept watching the pond. “Kind of.”

“Your uncle and his wife were there. She made some kind of meat dish.”

“What kind?”

“It was in a pot. Kind of sweet. Man, that was good.”

“Was that Japanese food?”

“I figured it was. She made it.”

Jeff tried to remember what had been on the table, but he couldn't come up with a name for it.

“I'll ask my uncle sometime.”

“He lives nearby, right?”

“Sagamihara. The next town over.”

“Tell him I want some more of that.”

“Think he’ll remember?”

“I remember it.”

His friend went back to watching the fish. Someone walked along the gravel path behind them; the footsteps faded toward the house.

“You know,” he said eventually, “back home you wouldn't have sat in a garden all afternoon.”

“It's quiet.”

“I know.”

“You're not getting up either.”

“My legs won't let me.”

Jeff stayed where he was. His friend leaned back against the bench and didn't say anything else for a while.

It was after three by the time they finally got up. They had barely made it down the road when his friend asked where they were going now.

“Hot springs,” Jeff said.

“There's more?”

“You get to sit down.”

“Sold.”

The sun was getting low when they made it back toward Takaosanguchi Station. At the bathhouse near the station, they left their shoes at the entrance and went into the changing room. His friend stopped in front of the lockers.

“Wait. Where are the swimsuits?”

“There aren't any.”

“We go in naked?”

“Yeah.”

“Everybody?”

“Everybody.”

He glanced around the room. “You could’ve warned me.”

“Would you have come?”

“...Probably.”

Inside, he followed Jeff to the washing area, washed himself, and eased into the bath. The water was hot enough to make him pull his shoulders up at first. A little later, he had both arms resting along the edge.

“Okay,” he said. “This is nice.”

“Told you.”

“My legs feel a lot better.”

“Mine too.”

Afterward they ordered beer in the bathhouse restaurant. His friend took a drink and set down his glass.

“That was a pretty good day.”

“Not a bad way to spend one.”

“We should do this every weekend.”

“Who's paying?”

“Was it that expensive?”

“The chairlift, the monkeys, the baths. And now beer.”

His friend looked at his glass.

“Okay, once a month.”

“Even that's pushing it.”

“Man, you're cheap.”

“You can pay next time.”

It was dark when they left. They took the train from Takaosanguchi and changed at Takao for the Chuo Line. As soon as his friend sat down, he closed his eyes.

Jeff nudged his shoulder as they approached Hachioji.

“Hey. Wake up. We have to change.”

“Huh? Already?”

“Keep sleeping and you'll end up at Tokyo Station.”

His friend opened his eyes.

“Aren't we in Tokyo now?”

“Tokyo Station. Come on.”

The train pulled into Hachioji. They got up and stepped onto the platform.

# Jeff — Episode 5: The Maze

"Push down right there."

Jeff pressed down on the suitcase lid while his friend tried to pull the zipper around it. The zipper was stuck halfway around, with a shirt sleeve poking out through the gap.

"It closed when you got here," Jeff said.

"Yeah. I don't know what happened to it."

Jeff tucked the sleeve back inside and kept his weight on the lid until the zipper closed. Together they carried the suitcase down the stairs of Jeff's building.

His friend was moving to Shinjuku that day. He'd arranged a furnished apartment through his Japanese language school before leaving the States. Orientation was at eleven, and the keys would be ready that afternoon.

On the way to Aihara Station, his friend asked, "Hey, what do you say when you get to work?"

"At the restaurant? Usually *otsukaresama desu*. Everybody says it."

"Ot-su-ka-re-sa-ma-desu."

"What for?"

"I'm gonna try it at school. Gotta know how to say something."

They kept walking.

They took the Yokohama Line to Hashimoto and switched to the Keio Line. With the suitcase between his feet, Jeff's friend checked the email from his school.

"Says it's a fifteen-minute walk from the west exit."

"Then we're fine."

"You know where that is?"

"We'll find it."

They got to Shinjuku a little after nine.

People poured off the train and headed for the stairs. Jeff followed them up, but before they reached the ticket gates, both of them stopped. Signs hung overhead pointing toward different exits. They found one for the west exit and followed it, only to reach another junction with more signs.

"This way, I think." Jeff started ahead.

His friend turned toward a different passage, pulling the suitcase behind him. "Wouldn't west be over there?"

"That's not what the sign says."

"Okay, lead the way."

They followed the underground passage, went up a flight of stairs, and came out onto a street. His friend checked the map on his phone. The blue dot showed them on the opposite side of the station from the school.

"Wrong side."

"Yeah."

"So what now?"

Jeff turned toward the stairs they'd just climbed. "Back down."

There were even more people underground now. The suitcase wheels kept catching on the seams in the floor. His friend lifted it for a few steps, then put it down and tried dragging it again.

"Hey. Didn't we pass that guy already?" His friend caught Jeff by the sleeve.

"Who?"

"The one with the black bag."

"Could be somebody who looks like him."

"No, I'm pretty sure it's the same guy."

"Maybe he thinks he's seen us before, too."

By the time his friend turned to check, the man had disappeared into the crowd.

"Seriously, how do you get out of this place?"

The passage split in two again. Jeff read the signs overhead.

"This is like a dungeon in a video game."

"Every time you turn, there's another hallway."

"And when you finally get out, you're in the wrong place."

# Jeff — Episode 6: Again

Jeff had three plates of salad lined up when the cook shouted at him.

「おい！　何やってんだ！」

「手で触るな！　落としたの戻すな！　捨てろ！」

“Today I was just making salads,” Jeff said.

“Salads?”

“Yeah, for an order.”

“And he yelled at you for that?”

“Exactly.”

“What the hell.”

They traded stories from other shifts. Working was easier when the cook had the day off, they agreed, and they checked the schedule to see when he'd be off again.

His coworker went back first. A little later, the Japanese coworker who'd once taken Jeff to Mount Takao on a day off came into the break room. “Got yelled at again today.”

“Where?”

“On the counter. Not the floor.”

“And?”

“I put it back. It looked clean.”

“Jeff, you can't do that.”

“But it was the counter.”

“Yeah.”

“When?”

“When I started work.”

“You were out front after that. Carrying plates, touching order slips, right?”

“Yeah.”

As he lifted the plates to carry them out, he glanced toward the cook.

Their eyes met.

The cook looked down again and went back to tossing the food in his pan.

Jeff carried out the salads.

# Jeff — Episode 7: The Chopstick Sleeve

Before the restaurant opened, Jeff was cutting cabbage.

He sliced the washed cabbage into thin strips and dropped it into a colander. A few pieces were still too big, so he picked them out and cut them again. Once it had drained, he packed the cabbage into containers and put them in the fridge. The coworker behind him had to wait until he shut the door to squeeze past.

「ごめんなさい」

「大丈夫。そこ狭いから」

Jeff went back to the sink. Lettuce was next. He was still slow with the knife, but by opening time he'd filled several containers with vegetables.

As customers came in, the order tickets started piling up. Jeff took vegetables from the fridge and used tongs to arrange them on plates. Between orders, he washed dishes until his Japanese coworker, who was helping out front, came into the kitchen.

「ジェフ、手が空いたら三番、片づけてもらえる？」

「はい」

After washing his hands, Jeff went out to help clear tables.

The table seated six. He started gathering up empty glasses and small plates, then stopped. At every place, a pair of chopsticks rested on a little piece of folded paper. All six were folded the same way.

He'd seen them now and then. He'd assumed they were paper decorations customers had left behind and thrown them away with the rest of the trash. Today there were six.

He moved a chopstick aside, picked up the folded paper, and showed it to the coworker, who was wiping down the counter.

“Hey, what’s this? I’ve seen these around.”

“Chopstick rests. We don’t put any out here, so some customers fold their wrappers into little rests.”

His coworker took the paper, set it on the table, and laid the chopsticks across it. The paper held its shape.

“You’re not really supposed to lay your chopsticks across a plate, either. It’s called *watashibashi*.”

“I do that all the time.”

“Yeah, I catch myself doing it sometimes.”

So that was what those little pieces of paper were for. Even where you set your chopsticks mattered in Japan.

He unfolded one edge of the paper and found the restaurant’s name.

“Oh, it’s a chopstick wrapper.”

“Yep.”

“I collect the nice ones. I like the different designs.”

“Huh.”

He turned the paper over. It was covered in creases.

“How do you fold one of these?”

“No idea. You should’ve asked whoever made it.”

He gathered the plates and chopsticks and cleared away all six folded wrappers.

At the next table, two large plates remained. One was still piled with edamame, and the salad on the other had barely been touched. There wasn't a single empty pod.

“They didn’t eat any of this.”

His coworker came over for the dishes and looked at the plates.

“You’re right. What a waste.”

“Why’d they order it?”

“Who knows? Maybe they thought they’d eat it.”

He picked up the plate of edamame.

“I would’ve eaten it.”

“All of it?”

“Yeah. I once had two bowls of katsudon at the airport. My friend had two, too.”

“Four bowls between you?”

“Yeah. We could barely move afterward.”

“You didn’t have to force yourself to eat that much.”

“I tried my best.”

“Some places used to box up whatever you couldn’t finish and let you take it home. Here, you either finish it or leave it.”

His coworker carried the glasses back to the kitchen. Jeff followed with the plates and emptied the untouched food into the waste container.

After closing, Jeff was cleaning the sink when his coworker brought him an unused chopstick wrapper.

“Want to try folding one?”

“Can I?”

“Sure. It’s just one. Can’t show you how, though.”

Jeff slipped it into a small pocket of his bag.

“Thanks.”

“You going to put that in your binder, too?”

“If I can fold it.”

“And if you can’t?”

Jeff thought about it.

“Might put it in anyway.”

Back at his apartment, Jeff set the new wrapper on the low table. He took two others with different designs out of his collection binder and laid them beside it. He tried to fold the new one into the shape he'd seen at the restaurant that afternoon.

He set a pair of disposable chopsticks on top. The paper flattened and the chopsticks rolled across the table.

His phone rang. It was his friend in Shinjuku.

“What are you doing?”

“Trying to make a chopstick rest.”

Jeff put the call on speaker and picked up the chopsticks.

“A chopstick rest?”

“You fold the wrapper into one. I saw it at work today.”

“Weren't you already collecting those wrappers?”

“Just the wrappers. I didn't know how to fold them.”

His friend laughed.

“So collecting other people's trash wasn't enough? Now you're making your own?”

“It's a chopstick rest.”

Jeff pressed down the middle of the paper and folded both ends again.

“Any luck?”

“Not yet.”

“Don't throw it away when you finally make one. After all that work.”

“I won't.”

After they hung up, Jeff set the chopsticks on it again. This time both stayed in place.

He leaned back to look at it. One fold slowly came undone, and a single chopstick fell off.

Jeff picked it up and slipped the wrinkled wrapper into an empty pocket of his collection binder.

# Jeff — Episode 8: The Book

On his day off, Jeff took the wrinkled chopstick wrapper out of his collection binder.

He'd tried folding it into a chopstick rest over and over the night before. Now that he'd flattened it out, he couldn't tell which crease he'd made first. He tried folding it again, but the paper opened up before he could even put the chopsticks on it.

He looked up how to make one on his phone. He played a video and paused it, but the person's fingers were covering the part he needed to see. When he went back a few seconds, he lost track of what the paper was supposed to look like before the fold.

Jeff closed the video. Maybe a book with step-by-step pictures would be easier.

He decided to try a bookstore in Hashimoto.

It was one stop from Aihara. He left the station, went into a shopping complex, and checked the directory for the bookstore.

While looking for the origami books, he spotted a familiar cover in the manga section.

It was a series his friend used to read all the time back in the States. The copies on the shelf were in Japanese.

A sample copy of volume one was on the shelf. Jeff picked it up. The dialogue was in Japanese, but it wasn't as hard to follow as he'd expected. There were kanji he didn't know, but the pictures helped him keep up.

He'd only meant to read a few pages, but he kept going. He stayed there by the shelf, turning back now and then to check something he'd missed.

When he checked his phone, he realized nearly an hour had passed.

He could actually read quite a bit of it.

He closed the book. Volumes two through ten were lined up beside it.

Maybe he'd send them to his friend.

He put the sample back and stacked up all ten volumes. They were heavier than he'd expected.

He was heading for the register when he remembered what he'd come for.

Carrying the manga, he checked the bookstore's section guide and went over to the craft section. Some books had paper cranes and flowers on their covers. Others showed how to fold boxes and small decorations. He flipped through several.

One of them had instructions for a chopstick rest.

It wasn't quite the shape he'd seen on the six-person table at the restaurant, but it used the same kind of chopstick wrapper. Beside the photo of the finished rest were diagrams showing each step.

He looked through the diagrams one by one. They seemed easy enough to follow.

Another page showed how to make a little container out of a paper napkin. He hesitated, then added the book to his stack.

At the register, he asked to have the ten manga volumes shipped to his friend's place in Shinjuku. The cashier handed him a shipping form. Jeff filled in the address, paid for the books and shipping, and left with the origami book in a bag.

Back at his apartment, he set the origami book and the chopstick wrapper on the low table. He opened to the page with the chopstick rest and lined up the paper with the first diagram.

Beside it was a word he didn't know: 谷折り.

He looked it up on his phone. *Valley fold.* On the same page was 山折り, which came up as *mountain fold*.

He folded the paper in half and opened it, then flipped it over to check the crease.

Following the arrows in the book, he got the direction wrong on the third diagram. When he tried to tuck a corner in, his finger slipped.

He unfolded the wrapper and started over.

The old creases kept getting in the way, but this time he managed to tuck the end into place. The little rest stood on the table without collapsing. He laid a pair of disposable chopsticks across it, and they stayed there.

He looked at it for a while, then compared his version with the picture in the book. His was wider, and one side was a little crooked. Still, the shape was close.

Jeff opened his collection binder. Inside were all the chopstick wrappers he'd saved, some printed with restaurant names, others with patterns.

He started to take one out, then stopped. It was a nice, clean wrapper without a single crease.

He put it back in the binder and moved his wrinkled chopstick rest to the edge of the table. Then he opened the book to the next page.

「はい、これでいいですよ」

“What?”

“But I thought she was nice. She helped me fix the forms.”

Jeff said nothing.

“And you meant it when you thanked her.”

“Yeah.”

“You thanked her for helping you fix the forms. There's nothing wrong with that.”

「ジェフ、7番テーブルの準備お願い」

Someone called from the dining room.

「はい」

Jeff picked up the plates and went out from behind the counter.

# Jeff — Episode 12: Tokyo at Night

# Jeff — Episode 13: The Mikoshi

「八時」

「早いですね」

「祭りだからな」

Eight in the morning. Jeff couldn't see why a festival needed such an early start.

「今は？」

「新しいの買った」

Jeff drank some. Sweat had already gathered at the back of his neck.

「は？」

「へえ」

# Jeff — Episode 14: My Town

"Good picture, isn't it?"

"Look at us. We look ridiculous."

"Let's take the lift down and try for a good one."

"Thought we were walking the whole way."

"We walked up. That's enough."

Jeff finished his rice ball.

They headed down past Yakuoin along the route they'd taken on their first visit. Wherever roots stuck out across the path, his friend slowed down.

"Going down's harder on my legs than going up."

"Not much farther to the lift."

"Didn't you just say that?"

They boarded the chairlift together. Partway down, his friend reached toward his pocket, then left his phone where it was.

"Where was that camera?"

"Should be coming up."

Jeff spotted it ahead.

"Keep your eyes open this time."

"And you face the camera."

They both faced the camera as the chair carried them past.

At the bottom, they found their picture on the display. This time they were both smiling, eyes open.

"I'm getting this one," Jeff said.

His friend took out his wallet.

"I want one too."

"You bought one last time."

"Yeah, but that was last time."

"Okay, let's get two."

They bought two copies of the same photo. His friend put his in his backpack, and Jeff slipped his away where it wouldn't bend.

"I might put this up in my room when I get home."

"What about the old one?"

"That one's going up too."

"With us looking like that?"

"It's a good picture."

After they left the lift station, Jeff checked the train times on his phone.

"Want to go to the same hot spring as last time?"

"Found another place. Let's try that one."

"Is it far?"

"We take a train, then walk a bit."

"No more hills, okay?"

"I think it's flat."

After the train ride, they walked to the bathhouse. His friend looked around as he took off his shoes.

"Think we'll have the place to ourselves at this hour?"

"Don't count on it."

There were only a few people at the washing stations when they went in.

"See? Hardly anyone here."

"Not exactly a private bath."

After their baths, they ate a late lunch at the bathhouse restaurant. When his friend finished his cold green tea, he glanced at the clock.

"I might go back in."

"We've got time."

They rested for a while, then went back for another soak.

Coming out the second time, his friend pointed toward the restaurant.

"I could go for a beer."

"We just ate there."

"Just a drink this time."

They went back to their table and ordered a beer each.

"That felt good," he said, setting down his glass. "Man, I could live here. I'd come every day."

"Live at the bathhouse?"

"Near it."

"You'd come every day?"

"Even just on my days off would be nice."

He took another drink.

"Wouldn't mind more days like this."

"You planning to climb a mountain every weekend?"

"The mountain part? Once in a while."

They stayed at the table after finishing their beers. By the time they left, about three hours had passed since they'd arrived, and the bathhouse was busier.

His friend fell asleep almost as soon as he sat down on the train.

As the train neared the station where they had to change, Jeff tapped his friend on the shoulder.

"Hey, wake up. We have to change here."

He opened his eyes.

"Are we there?"

"We're changing trains."

He grabbed his backpack, stood up, and followed Jeff out.

---

On Sunday, Jeff was in front of the fish tank when his friend woke up.

"Looking at them again?"

"One of the eggs is gone."

His friend crouched down beside him and examined the plants.

"Maybe it's stuck somewhere else."

"Could be."

Neither of them could find it. Jeff stood, and his friend got up beside him.

"Come on, let's get lunch. I'm starving."

After his friend packed his things for the trip home, they set out toward the station.

Just around the corner, a man carrying a shopping bag raised his hand and called Jeff by name. Jeff passed him most mornings on his way to the station.

「おう、ジェフ。この前はお疲れさん」

「お疲れさまです」

「また祭りも頼むよ」

The man laughed and went on his way.

His friend watched the man go.

"You know that guy?"

"We carried the mikoshi together at the festival."

"You joined a festival out here, too?"

"Yeah. My shoulder was killing me afterward."

He gave Jeff a quick pat on the shoulder.

"Worse than the mountain?"

"Way worse."

Outside the supermarket, the woman who usually worked the register saw Jeff and called to him from near the entrance.

「あ、ジェフ！　今日はお友達？」

「はい」

「いつものラーメン、新しいの入ったよ」

「ほんとですか。帰りに見ます」

「またね」

Jeff waved, and she went back inside.

"They even know you at the grocery store?"

"I go there all the time."

"She even knows what you buy."

"I buy the same stuff every time."

Before they reached the main road, someone called Jeff's name from the sidewalk across the street.

「ジェフー！」

It was the regular from the izakaya. He raised a hand without breaking stride, and Jeff waved back.

His friend stopped.

"What are you, famous around here?"

"No. They're just people I see all the time."

"I've been here two days, and nobody's called my name once."

"You don't live here."

"Fair point."

He started walking again, then turned to Jeff.

"So what's for lunch?"

"There's a katsudon place nearby."

"Katsudon again?"

"We can go somewhere else."

"No, katsudon's fine. Just one bowl this time."

"One's enough for me too."

"Wasn't that the plan last time, too?"

Jeff laughed and pointed farther down the street.

"This way. Just a little farther."

"After all that walking yesterday?"

"It's close."

His friend adjusted the strap on his backpack and followed Jeff.
